ALEX — RED FLAG LIBRARY
CATEGORY 5: TRACTION RED FLAGS
Version 1.0. Built: April 2026.
15 flags. Apply mechanically to every deal submission.
These flags are observable from a submission without a meeting.
Two analysts applying these rules reach the same conclusion.
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

APPLICATION RULE:
For every deal submission, Alex checks all 15 flags before completing
the screening note. Flags are not discretionary — if the signal is
present, the flag is raised. Flags are then weighed in aggregate.
One CRITICAL flag = automatic deep investigation required.
Two or more HIGH flags = note this in the Risk Assessment section.
Traction flags are the most immediate signals Alex has access to —
they reflect what is happening right now in the business, not what
happened at founding or what may happen at scale. Weight them accordingly.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR01: GROWTH RATE DECLINING — THREE OR MORE CONSECUTIVE QUARTERS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
MoM or YoY revenue growth rate has declined in each of the last 3 or more consecutive
quarters — not a single-quarter dip explained by seasonality or a one-time event,
but a sustained, consistent deceleration. Detectable when quarterly ARR additions
show a declining trend over 3 or more periods: Q1 +£300K, Q2 +£250K, Q3 +£190K,
Q4 +£140K. The absolute growth may still be positive — the flag is the direction
of change in the growth rate, not the absolute level.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A sustained growth deceleration is the most reliable leading indicator of a company
that will fail to achieve its forecast. Three consecutive quarters of deceleration
is not noise — it is a trend. The causes are predictable: (1) the early-adopter
market is saturated and the company has not yet broken into the mainstream buyer pool;
(2) the product is not delivering sufficient retention to maintain compounding —
churn is increasing as the customer base grows beyond the founder network; (3) the
sales motion that worked for the first 30 customers is not working for customers
31 through 100 — the playbook needs to change but has not. The financial consequence
of a decelerating growth rate is a compounding problem: a company growing at 12% MoM
that decelerates to 7% MoM within 12 months will have 40% less ARR than its baseline
forecast implies — which means it will run out of runway or fail its next fundraise
on the timeline the current raise assumes.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Growth rate has declined in each of the last 4 or more consecutive quarters AND
the most recent quarter's growth rate is below 5% MoM (for a pre-Series A company)
or below 20% YoY (for a Series A company) AND the company's financial model projects
re-acceleration without a specific identified driver — the model assumes a recovery
that the data does not support.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) One quarter of the three is explained by a specific, non-recurring event —
a key customer delayed renewal, a seasonal market pause, a product release delayed
a major deal by one quarter — and the deceleration is two true trend quarters plus
one explained outlier.
(b) The deceleration was driven by a deliberate strategic decision — moving upmarket
to enterprise (which lengthens sales cycles and temporarily depresses new customer
additions), or removing a low-quality free tier — and the new growth motion is showing
early signs of re-acceleration in the most recent data.
(c) The company is at an early stage (below £500K ARR) where the growth rate is
volatile due to the small denominator and individual deal timing effects are large.

VERIFICATION:
Request quarterly ARR data for the last 6 quarters broken into: gross new ARR,
expansion ARR, churned ARR, net new ARR. Calculate the MoM or QoQ net new ARR
trend. Ask: "Your net new ARR has been declining for [X] quarters — what is the
primary driver and what specific change have you made or plan to make to reverse it?"
The specificity of the answer — is there a concrete driver identified and a concrete
response planned — is the primary signal.

PATTERN LESSON:
When net new ARR has declined for three or more consecutive quarters, calculate the
implied ARR at the current deceleration rate 12 months forward and compare it to
the financial model — if the model assumes re-acceleration without a specific identified
driver, the forecast is not credible.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR02: COHORT QUALITY DECLINING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
More recent customer cohorts — customers acquired in the last 12 months — show
measurably worse retention at the same age as older cohorts. Detectable from:
cohort retention tables that show newer cohort lines below older cohort lines at
equivalent time points; a company that states NRR across all cohorts while NRR
within recent cohorts alone is materially lower; or a company that reports a
declining first-renewal retention rate as more customers reach their 12-month mark.

COHORT QUALITY CALCULATION:
For each acquisition cohort (e.g. Q1 2024, Q2 2024), calculate ARR retained at
12 months as a percentage of ARR at signing. Compare the 12-month retention rates
across cohorts. A declining trend — cohort 2024 Q1 retains 85%, Q2 retains 79%,
Q3 retains 71% — is cohort quality deterioration regardless of what the blended
NRR figure shows.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Cohort quality deterioration is more alarming than a low current NRR because it
predicts future NRR — the blended NRR figure today includes all cohorts, many of
which are healthy early customers. As the lower-quality recent cohorts grow to
represent a larger share of the total customer base, the blended NRR will decline.
A company that reports 112% NRR today but has recent cohort retention declining
from 85% to 70% will likely be reporting sub-100% NRR in 12–18 months. The causes
of cohort quality decline: (1) the ICP has shifted — the company is acquiring customers
who are a worse fit for the product than early adopters were; (2) the product has
degraded relative to expectations — more customers, more churn sources, product quality
is not keeping up with the retention promise; (3) the sales motion is creating
unrealistic expectations — new customers are being closed on promises the product
cannot keep.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Three or more consecutive cohorts show declining 12-month retention AND the most
recent cohort's 12-month retention is more than 15 percentage points below the
oldest cohort measured AND the company does not have a documented explanation for
the deterioration or a remediation plan in execution.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The deterioration is attributed to a specific, bounded ICP expansion — the company
deliberately acquired 20 customers outside its core ICP in one quarter to test a
new segment. Those customers retained at 65% while the core ICP retains at 88%.
The cohort data shows the experiment and its result; the core ICP cohort data is stable.
(b) Cohort data is sparse — fewer than 3 full cohorts have reached 12-month maturity
and the variation is within statistical noise for small cohort sizes.
(c) The retention deterioration is fully explained by customers lost in the first
90 days due to an onboarding problem that has since been identified and fixed —
the at-12-month retention of customers who survive 90 days is stable.

VERIFICATION:
Request a cohort retention table: customers acquired by quarter for the last 6 quarters,
with their ARR retained at 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months. Plot the 12-month
retention line across cohorts. Ask: "Have your recent customer cohorts been retaining
at the same rate as your earlier cohorts at the same point in their lifecycle?" If
the answer is yes, request the data to confirm. If the answer is no, ask: "What
do you attribute the change to?"

PATTERN LESSON:
When NRR is reported as a blended figure across all cohorts, request cohort-level
retention data for the last 6 acquisition cohorts — declining retention in recent
cohorts predicts declining blended NRR with a 12–18 month lag that will not be
visible in current headline metrics.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR03: NET REVENUE RETENTION BELOW 100%
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH (NRR 90–100%) | CRITICAL (NRR below 90%)

SIGNAL:
NRR — calculated on the same cohort of customers at the start and end of a 12-month
period, excluding any customer acquired during the period — is below 100%. Meaning:
the ARR generated by existing customers at the end of the period is less than the
ARR they generated at the start, even before accounting for any new customers added.

CALCULATION CONFIRMATION:
NRR = (Starting cohort ARR + Expansion ARR − Contraction ARR − Churned ARR) ÷ Starting cohort ARR
If NRR is below 100%, the existing customer base is net shrinking. All new customer
ARR is required to offset the decline in existing ARR before net growth can begin.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Sub-100% NRR means the company is on a leaky bucket model: it must continuously
acquire new customers just to maintain flat total ARR. Every £1 of new customer ARR
acquired replaces a £1 of existing ARR that has left — the company is running to
stand still. The compounding consequence: as the customer base grows, the absolute
amount of ARR to replace grows with it. A company with £5M ARR and 95% NRR must
add £250K of new ARR quarterly just to maintain flat ARR — before any growth occurs.
At 90% NRR with £10M ARR, the required quarterly replacement is £1M — which demands
a sales machine that is running full-speed without producing any visible ARR growth.
Below 90% NRR, the bucket has a structural hole that new customer acquisition alone
cannot fill at realistic growth rates.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
NRR is below 90% AND the customer base has more than 30 logos (so the figure is
statistically meaningful, not distorted by 1–2 large churns) AND the trend in NRR
is flat or declining AND the company is projecting NRR improvement without a specific
structural change to the retention motion — the model assumes a recovery that the
retention data does not support.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) NRR is between 95–100% for a company at pre-seed or seed with fewer than 15
customers — a single logo churn or contraction creates a below-100% figure that
is not statistically representative.
(b) The sub-100% NRR is entirely attributable to a known, bounded event — one large
customer who churned due to their own business failure (bankruptcy, acquisition,
shutdown) rather than product dissatisfaction — and the rest of the cohort shows
stable retention.
(c) NRR has been sub-100% historically but a specific remediation action (a customer
success hire, a product fix, a pricing model change) was implemented in the most recent
quarter and early data shows improvement.

VERIFICATION:
Request NRR decomposed into: gross renewal rate, expansion rate, contraction rate,
and churn rate for the last 4 quarters. Calculate NRR from components. If the
company provides a single NRR figure without decomposition, request the component
data. The most important component is gross renewal rate — below 80% gross renewal
is a product failure signal; above 90% gross renewal with sub-100% NRR is a
low-expansion signal (different problem, different fix).

PATTERN LESSON:
When NRR is below 100%, decompose into gross renewal rate and net expansion rate
separately — below-80% gross renewal signals product failure; 85%+ gross renewal
with sub-100% NRR signals an expansion motion problem — each requires a different
response and carries a different severity.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR04: LOGO CHURN ABOVE SECTOR BENCHMARK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
Annual logo churn — calculated as logos churned ÷ logos at the start of the period —
exceeds the sector benchmark for the business model type and stage. Note: this flag
requires correct denominator calculation (see below). A company that grew from 50
to 80 customers and lost 8 has a logo churn rate of 16% (8÷50), not 10% (8÷80).

SECTOR LOGO CHURN BENCHMARKS (ANNUAL):
Enterprise software (ACV >£100K): STRONG <3% | CONCERN >7% | FAIL >12%
Mid-market SaaS (ACV £10–100K):   STRONG <5% | CONCERN >10% | FAIL >15%
SMB SaaS (ACV <£10K):             STRONG <10% | CONCERN >20% | FAIL >30%
Consumer subscription (annual):   STRONG <10% | CONCERN >25% | FAIL >40%
Marketplace (seller side):        STRONG <15% | CONCERN >30% | FAIL >50%

WHAT IT MEANS:
Logo churn above the sector benchmark means the company is losing customers at
a rate that its sector peers sustain below. This is not merely a financial signal —
it is a product signal. Logo churn above benchmark indicates one or more of: the
ICP is wrong (acquiring customers who should not have been acquired); the product
does not deliver on its value promise (customers try it and leave); the customer
success motion is insufficient (customers are not supported through adoption);
or competitive alternatives are improving and customers are switching at renewal.
High logo churn also has a non-financial consequence: every churned customer is a
failed reference, a potential negative-word-of-mouth source, and a case study the
company cannot use. At scale, high logo churn creates a reputational headwind that
slows new customer acquisition even as the company invests more in marketing.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Annual logo churn exceeds the FAIL threshold for the sector AND the company has
more than 20 customers (so the figure is statistically meaningful) AND churn has
been above the FAIL threshold for 2 or more consecutive measurement periods AND
the company does not have a documented CS intervention programme targeting the
at-risk customer segments.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) Logo churn is above benchmark but NRR is above 100% — high logo churn in SMB
combined with strong expansion in enterprise can produce healthy NRR. The logo churn
is a distribution mix issue, not a retention crisis.
(b) Logo churn has declined materially in the most recent measurement period due to
an identified and implemented remediation (a CS hire, an onboarding redesign, a
product fix) — the trend is moving in the right direction with a specific causal explanation.
(c) A one-time cohort event — a customer segment acquired under a promotional pricing
campaign that was not sustainable — has been identified and removed from the cohort,
and the underlying logo churn is within benchmark.

VERIFICATION:
Request the logo churn calculation with the correct denominator: logos at start of
period, not end of period. Ask: "Of your customers 12 months ago, how many are still
active today?" Calculate logo churn from this figure. Then ask: "What were the
primary reasons given by customers who churned?" The stated reasons — price, product,
competitor, own business failure — determine whether churn is addressable.

PATTERN LESSON:
When logo churn is disclosed or calculable, verify the denominator is the starting
customer count not the ending count, and then compare the result to the sector
benchmark — denominator manipulation is the most common way logo churn is
understated, and the corrected figure often exceeds the benchmark by 5–10 percentage points.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR05: ALL GROWTH FROM ONE UNOWNED CHANNEL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
100% or near-100% of new customer acquisition comes from a single channel that the
company does not own or control — a single referral partner, a single marketplace
or app store, a single API integration partner, a single community or platform —
such that if that channel changed its terms, reduced its distribution, or was acquired,
new customer acquisition would halt. Detectable from: all named customer sources in
the submission come from the same partner; the submission references a strategic
partnership as the exclusive source of growth; the GTM section describes a single
channel without any diversification plan.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Channel concentration is the distribution equivalent of customer concentration —
the business is structurally dependent on one relationship it does not control.
The specific failure modes: (1) the channel partner changes its revenue share terms,
referral fee, or integration policies — the economics of customer acquisition change
overnight; (2) the channel partner launches a competing product and deprioritises
or terminates the relationship — customer acquisition drops to zero without warning;
(3) the channel partner is acquired by a strategic competitor — the acquirer terminates
the partnership; (4) the channel experiences external disruption (app store policy
change, platform algorithm change, partner financial difficulty) — the company inherits
the disruption with no alternative acquisition source. The company that grows entirely
through one unowned channel has not built a GTM capability — it has built a dependency.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
More than 85% of all new customer acquisition in the last 12 months came from a
single unowned channel AND the channel has notified the company of a policy or
commercial term change in the last 6 months AND the company has no documented
plan to build a second acquisition channel with a specific investment and timeline.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The single channel is the deliberate go-to-market for the early stage — the company
is focused on proving the channel before diversifying. The concentration is intentional
and the channel is performing well. A second channel is planned and resourced once
the first is proven, not abandoned.
(b) The company owns the economic relationship with the end customer even if the
acquisition came through a partner — meaning the partner is a referral source but
not a gatekeeper to renewal, expansion, or ongoing relationship.
(c) The channel partner relationship is governed by an exclusive contract with minimum
volume commitments and a penalty for termination — the concentration risk is mitigated
by contractual protection for the investment horizon.

VERIFICATION:
Request the new customer acquisition source breakdown for the last 12 months by channel.
Ask: "If [named channel] stopped sending you customers tomorrow, what would your
new customer acquisition rate be in the following month?" A company with genuine
channel diversification will describe alternatives. A company with channel dependency
will describe a crisis scenario.

PATTERN LESSON:
When all new customer acquisition is attributable to one channel, verify whether
the company owns the end-customer relationship or whether the channel partner
does — a company that loses the channel and loses the customer simultaneously
has no business without the partner.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR06: GROWTH RATE SPIKE WITHOUT EXPLANATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: MEDIUM

SIGNAL:
Revenue or ARR in a specific quarter is materially higher than the growth trend —
typically 2x or more of the average quarterly ARR addition — with no explanation
offered in the submission for the spike. The spike is either the most recent quarter
(which the company is using as the basis for its growth narrative) or a historical
quarter that distorts the reported YoY growth rate.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A growth spike without explanation is not necessarily negative — it may represent
a large enterprise deal closing, a viral moment, a partnership activation, or a
press event driving inbound demand. But when the spike is unexplained, Alex cannot
assess whether it is: (a) repeatable — a product-led flywheel that will continue;
(b) one-time — a single large deal or event that will not recur; or (c) a metric
manipulation — ARR recognised from a multi-year contract in the spike quarter
that should have been recognised ratably, creating an artificial spike in one
quarter and a corresponding trough in subsequent quarters. The most common misleading
use of a growth spike: a company that signs a 3-year £900K contract and recognises
£900K as ARR in the signing quarter, then reports "ARR tripled last quarter" without
disclosing that the prior quarters' growth rates were flat.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The growth spike represents more than 30% of total cumulative ARR AND is in the
most recent quarter AND the company's forward projections assume the spike-level
quarterly addition as the new run rate AND no explanation for the spike is provided —
the growth story and the financial model are built on a number whose repeatability
cannot be assessed.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The spike is explicitly explained in the submission — a named enterprise deal,
a named partnership activation, a named press event — and the company's projection
treats the spike as a one-time addition rather than the new run rate.
(b) The spike reflects a genuine product-led viral moment — a feature launch that
drove significant inbound — and usage data supports that the acquired users are
retained and active, suggesting durability.
(c) The spike was in a historical quarter that is now 12+ months old — it affects
the YoY comparison but not the current growth trajectory assessment.

VERIFICATION:
Request the quarterly ARR breakdown for the last 8 quarters. Identify the spike quarter.
Ask: "What drove the unusually high ARR addition in [quarter]? Is that growth driver
still active, and what is your expectation for a similar quarter in the next 12 months?"
The answer to whether the driver is still active determines whether the spike is
structural or one-time.

PATTERN LESSON:
When a single quarter's ARR addition is more than 2x the trailing average, ask for
the specific driver and assess whether it is repeatable before using that quarter's
growth rate as the basis for forward projections.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR07: USAGE METRICS NOT PROVIDED AT SERIES A
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
A company at Series A — defined as £1M+ ARR or a disclosed Series A raise — provides
detailed ARR and growth metrics but cannot or will not provide any product usage data:
no daily or monthly active users, no feature adoption rates, no session frequency,
no engagement metrics. Note: this flag is the traction-category companion to Product
Flag P09 — they address the same omission from different analytical angles. Both can
apply simultaneously.

WHAT IT MEANS:
ARR is a contractual measure. Usage is the behavioural measure. The gap between what
customers have agreed to pay and how actively they are using the product is the best
forward indicator of retention. A Series A company that has built a billing infrastructure
sufficient to invoice £1M+ in ARR but has not built the product instrumentation to
know how often customers use the product has prioritised revenue extraction over
product health monitoring — which is a specific choice that predicts specific problems.
Three outcomes from non-measurement: (1) customers who are not using the product are
not detected before renewal — the first signal is the non-renewal, not a preventable
warning 90 days before; (2) the product team cannot identify which features are
driving value and which are unused — prioritisation is based on assumption, not data;
(3) the NRR figure cannot be explained by reference to usage — when NRR declines,
the team does not know whether it is driven by usage decline, product failure, or
competitive displacement.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The company has more than £1M ARR AND more than 20 customers AND has been operating
for more than 18 months AND cannot provide any usage metric for any customer AND
does not have product analytics tooling in place (Mixpanel, Amplitude, Segment,
or custom instrumentation) — at this stage, the absence of measurement is not a
tooling gap, it is a product management philosophy that will compound into a
retention problem.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The product is an enterprise software with a small number of large accounts
where usage is monitored through CS engagement logs and QBR reporting rather
than automated instrumentation — the company can describe specific usage behaviour
for each account even without a DAU/MAU dashboard.
(b) The product is a regulated healthcare or financial services product where automated
usage tracking requires patient or customer consent and the implementation is in
progress — the absence is a compliance timeline, not an oversight.
(c) The company has recently implemented product analytics (within the last 60 days)
and early data is available — the absence was historical but the gap is being addressed.

VERIFICATION:
Ask: "What is your DAU/MAU ratio? What percentage of your customers logged in at
least once in the last 30 days? Which features do your most active customers use
most? And which customers are at risk based on usage data?" A company with product
instrumentation answers all four questions immediately. A company without it gives
qualitative answers to all four. The quality of the answers reveals the measurement
maturity independent of what the numbers say.

PATTERN LESSON:
When usage metrics are absent alongside ARR data at Series A, ask for the specific
tooling the company uses to track product engagement — the name of the tool and
the date it was implemented tells Alex whether the omission is structural or transitional.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR08: CAC PAYBACK PERIOD EXTENDING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
CAC payback period — calculated as CAC ÷ (ACV × gross margin) expressed in months —
has increased quarter-on-quarter for 2 or more consecutive quarters. Detectable when
the company discloses CAC and gross margin across multiple periods, or when the ratio
of sales and marketing spend to net new ARR is increasing over time. Can also be
inferred when the company reports rising CAC or declining ACV or declining gross
margin, any of which would extend payback arithmetically.

PAYBACK PERIOD CALCULATION:
CAC payback (months) = CAC ÷ (Monthly Gross Profit per customer)
Monthly Gross Profit per customer = (ACV × gross margin) ÷ 12
If CAC is rising, ACV is falling, or gross margin is compressing — or any combination
of the three — payback period extends. Alex should calculate this from component data
if the payback period is not directly disclosed.

WHAT IT MEANS:
An extending payback period means the company is becoming less efficient at recovering
its customer acquisition cost — the unit economics of the business are moving in the
wrong direction. Three specific failure modes that an extending payback reveals: (1)
CAC spiral in early formation (Financial Flag F10) — paid acquisition channels are
saturating and each additional customer costs more to acquire; (2) ACV compression —
the company is closing deals at lower ACV, either through discounting or by shifting
to lower-tier customers, reducing the revenue per unit of acquisition cost; (3) gross
margin compression — COGS are rising as the business scales (AI inference costs,
implementation costs, support costs) reducing the profit earned per customer per month.
Any one of the three extends payback — all three simultaneously is a structural unit
economics failure.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Payback period has extended by more than 50% over 4 or more consecutive quarters AND
the current payback period exceeds the sector threshold (24 months for SMB SaaS,
36 months for enterprise) AND the company cannot identify a specific driver and
a specific remediation — the efficiency regression is unexplained and unchecked.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) Payback extension is entirely attributable to a deliberate move upmarket — ACV
is increasing but the sales cycle is longer, which extends the payback calculation
temporarily while the higher ACV compounds. The absolute LTV is improving even as
payback extends — the unit economics are better, not worse.
(b) Extension is due to a planned increase in sales team capacity ahead of a pipeline
that is 90 days from closing — a temporary increase in CAC numerator that will
normalise when the new hires reach quota.
(c) Payback period has extended but remains within the sector benchmark — the extension
is visible in the trend but not yet at a problematic absolute level.

VERIFICATION:
Request quarterly CAC, ACV, and gross margin for the last 6 quarters. Calculate the
payback period for each quarter. Ask: "Has your customer acquisition cost changed in
the last 12 months? If so, what has driven the change?" Then ask about ACV and gross
margin separately. The combination of the three data points reveals whether payback
extension is a pricing issue, a cost issue, or an efficiency issue.

PATTERN LESSON:
When payback period is disclosed or calculable and has extended for 2 or more consecutive
quarters, decompose the extension into its CAC, ACV, and gross margin components —
the source of the extension determines whether it is a sales efficiency problem,
a pricing problem, or a margin problem, and each requires a different response.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR09: EXPANSION REVENUE CONCENTRATED IN ONE OR TWO ACCOUNTS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
NRR is above 100% — which appears positive — but the expansion ARR driving it is
concentrated in one or two large accounts. If those accounts are removed from the
NRR calculation, NRR falls below 100%. Detectable when: one customer appears in
expansion case studies disproportionately; the company's total expansion ARR in a
period is close to the ACV of a single named account expansion; or the company's
customer count is growing but total ARR is growing faster, with one account responsible
for most of the excess.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A 115% NRR that is entirely driven by one account expanding from £200K to £600K ARR
is not evidence of a healthy land-and-expand motion — it is evidence of one exceptional
relationship that has disguised what may be a flat or declining customer base. When
that account's expansion is complete, NRR will revert to the underlying base rate —
which may be sub-100%. This flag is the expansion equivalent of revenue concentration
(Financial Flag F01): the metric looks healthy in aggregate but the underlying
distribution is fragile. Additionally, the expanding account may be approaching
its maximum spend — there is a ceiling on how much any single customer will pay —
and when that ceiling is reached, the expansion contributor that has been inflating
NRR disappears from the metric entirely.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Excluding the top 2 expansion accounts, NRR falls below 95% AND the company has
more than 15 customers (so the exclusion is statistically meaningful) AND the top
2 accounts represent more than 50% of total expansion ARR in the period — the
headline NRR is not representative of the underlying customer base health.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The concentrated expansion is in the earliest cohort of enterprise accounts —
those customers are inherently the largest spenders because they were the first to
see value and have had the longest time to expand. The expansion concentration is
a cohort timing effect, not a structural concentration.
(b) There are 3 or more accounts with meaningful expansion in the period — the
concentration in one or two is proportional but not exclusive. Other accounts are
also expanding, even if at smaller scale.
(c) The concentrated expansion reflects a genuine step-change in the account's
use case — the account expanded to new departments, new geographies, or a new
product module that will now be repeatable with other accounts.

VERIFICATION:
Request expansion ARR by account for the last 4 quarters. Calculate NRR excluding
the top 2 expansion accounts. Ask: "If you remove your two largest expanding accounts
from the NRR calculation, what is the NRR for the rest of the base?" A company that
has a healthy broad-based expansion motion will answer confidently. A company whose
NRR is concentrated will either not have the calculation ready or will reveal a
materially lower underlying figure.

PATTERN LESSON:
When NRR is above 110%, calculate what NRR would be excluding the top 2 expansion
accounts — a high headline NRR concentrated in one or two accounts is a concentration
risk, not an expansion motion, and the two require entirely different responses.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR10: PILOT-TO-PAID CONVERSION RATE BELOW 50%
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
The company runs product pilots or proof-of-concept periods as part of its sales
motion, and fewer than 50% of completed pilots convert to a paid contract. Detectable
when: the submission mentions pilots as part of the sales process and describes the
number of current pilots; the conversion rate is stated; or the ratio of pipeline
described as "in pilot" to closed deals implies a conversion rate below 50%.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A sub-50% pilot-to-paid conversion rate means the majority of the company's most
advanced sales-stage opportunities do not become customers. This has two interpretations:
(1) The product does not deliver sufficient proof of value in the pilot period —
the customer evaluates it, does not see the ROI they expected, and does not convert.
This is a product-market fit signal.
(2) The sales process is not designed to create conversion commitment — pilots are
being run without a success criterion, a commercial agreement, or a conversion plan.
The customer uses the pilot, finds value, but has no obligation or incentive to
convert — and the sales team has not created one. This is a sales process signal.
Both signals require different responses, but both indicate that the company's most
resource-intensive sales investment (implementing a pilot) is producing a negative
outcome in more than half of cases — a direct hit on sales efficiency.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Pilot conversion rate is below 30% AND the company has completed more than 10 pilots
(so the figure is statistically meaningful) AND no root cause analysis of failed
conversions has been conducted or documented — more than 7 in 10 pilots are failing
and the company does not know why.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) Pilots include a paid component — even a small fee (£2–5K for a 90-day pilot)
— which confirms buyer intent. Paid pilots convert at 70–85%; free pilots convert
at 30–50%. If the company is running paid pilots, the sub-50% threshold is applied
at a higher base conversion expectation.
(b) The conversion rate is calculated on pilots that have formally ended — many
pilots that have not yet converted are still active and within their evaluation period.
The at-completion conversion rate is a different figure from the at-point-in-time
conversion rate.
(c) Each pilot failure has been documented with a reason — lost to competitor (competitive
signal), not enough budget (timing signal), or decision-maker changed (champion dependency
signal) — and the company is using the failure data to improve pilot design.

VERIFICATION:
Request a pilot log: all pilots conducted in the last 18 months, their outcome (converted,
failed to convert, ongoing), and for each failed conversion, the stated reason. Ask:
"Of pilots completed in the last 12 months, what percentage converted to a paid contract?
And what were the most common reasons for the non-conversions?" The reasons reveal
whether the problem is product, process, or pricing.

PATTERN LESSON:
When pilots are part of the sales motion, request the pilot log with outcomes and
stated conversion reasons before accepting the pilot pipeline as evidence of commercial
traction — a pipeline full of active pilots with a historical conversion rate below
50% is not a commercial pipeline, it is a prospect list with an expensive evaluation model.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR11: SALES CYCLE EXTENDING QUARTER-ON-QUARTER
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: MEDIUM

SIGNAL:
Average time from first qualified contact to signed contract has increased in each
of the last 2 or more consecutive quarters. Detectable when: the company discloses
sales cycle length across multiple periods; the submission mentions that deals are
"taking longer to close"; the pipeline age data shows deals that have been open for
materially longer than the stated average; or the number of deals in pipeline has
grown without a corresponding increase in closed deals.

WHAT IT MEANS:
An extending sales cycle means each unit of sales capacity is producing less output
in a given period. A salesperson who could close 2 deals per quarter when the cycle
was 3 months closes 1 deal per quarter when the cycle extends to 6 months — for the
same headcount cost. The causes of sales cycle extension in order of frequency:
(1) The company is moving upmarket without adapting its sales process — enterprise
buyers require more stakeholders, more legal review, and more security diligence than
the mid-market motion the company was built for; (2) the market is becoming more
cautious — buyers are taking longer to approve software spend and adding more
stakeholder checkpoints; (3) the product is not compelling enough to drive urgency —
buyers are evaluating but not deciding because the pain is not sufficient to justify
the effort of procurement; (4) a specific procurement friction has emerged (security
review, contract terms dispute) that is common across multiple deals.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Sales cycle has extended by more than 50% over 4 or more consecutive quarters AND
the company has not identified the specific cause AND the financial model assumes
a sales cycle that is 30% shorter than the current measured average — the model
is built on a sales productivity assumption that the current sales data contradicts.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) Sales cycle extension is entirely explained by a deliberate move upmarket — the
company is now selling to enterprise buyers who have longer cycles, but ACV has
increased proportionally with the cycle length, maintaining sales productivity
in revenue terms even if not in deal count terms.
(b) Extension is in a specific deal stage (legal review, security review) that the
company has identified and is addressing — hiring a dedicated legal or compliance
resource to manage the bottleneck.
(c) The extension is within the range of normal variation for the company's stage
and sector — a 1-month extension in a 9-month enterprise cycle is noise.

VERIFICATION:
Request pipeline data with deal open dates. Ask: "What was your average sales cycle
12 months ago versus today? And which stage of the sales process has extended most —
initial evaluation, procurement, legal, or security?" The bottleneck stage identifies
the fix. An extending evaluation stage is a product or positioning problem; an extending
legal stage is a contract or security infrastructure problem.

PATTERN LESSON:
When sales cycle length has extended across consecutive quarters, identify the specific
stage in the pipeline where deals are stalling before accepting the extension as an
unavoidable market condition — stage-level bottleneck analysis reveals a fixable
process problem more often than a structural market problem.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR12: MAGIC NUMBER BELOW 0.5
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
The Magic Number — calculated as Net New ARR in the quarter × 4, divided by sales
and marketing spend in the prior quarter — is below 0.5. This means the company
is generating less than £0.50 of annualised new ARR for every £1 of sales and
marketing spend. Directly observable when both net new ARR and S&M spend are
disclosed; estimable from headcount and ARR data when not directly stated.

MAGIC NUMBER CALCULATION:
Magic Number = (Net New ARR Q2 × 4) ÷ S&M Spend Q1
Where Net New ARR = Ending ARR − Beginning ARR + Churned ARR
A Magic Number above 0.75 is STRONG. Below 0.5 indicates the sales and marketing
investment is not efficiently converting to recurring revenue.

WHAT IT MEANS:
The Magic Number measures sales efficiency — how much annualised revenue is generated
per unit of sales investment. Below 0.5 means the company is spending £2 or more
to generate £1 of annualised new revenue. This has a direct implication for burn
efficiency: a company with a Magic Number of 0.3 must spend £3.30 of capital to
generate £1 of ARR, which means at a 5x ARR valuation, every £1 of ARR generated
requires £3.30 of capital investment — significantly destroying the return profile
before any other costs are considered. The Magic Number below 0.5 also means that
doubling the sales and marketing spend will not double revenue — the efficiency
problem will compound.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Magic Number has been below 0.5 for 3 or more consecutive quarters AND is not
improving AND the company's financial model projects ARR growth that is only
achievable by increasing S&M spend further — the model is projecting growth through
an investment that has already demonstrated poor efficiency.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The Magic Number is temporarily depressed by a new sales team ramp — a company
that hired 5 salespeople in Q1 will show a very low Magic Number in Q2 (high S&M
spend, low new ARR from reps still in ramp) and a recovering Magic Number in Q3–Q4
as the new hires reach productivity.
(b) The Magic Number calculation excludes a significant inbound or PLG component —
if 40% of new ARR comes from organic inbound that has near-zero S&M cost, the
measured S&M spend overstates the cost of the total ARR addition.
(c) The company is at a very early stage (below £500K ARR) where the S&M spend is
building pipeline for deals that will close in a future quarter — the 90-day lag
between spend and close creates a temporarily low Magic Number that will normalise.

VERIFICATION:
Calculate Magic Number from the last 4 quarters of disclosed data. Ask: "What is
your Magic Number and how has it trended over the last 4 quarters?" If the company
does not know its Magic Number, this is itself a signal about financial discipline.
Ask for S&M spend by quarter alongside net new ARR by quarter to calculate independently.

PATTERN LESSON:
When the Magic Number is below 0.5, identify whether the cause is rising S&M spend
(efficiency problem) or declining net new ARR (product or market problem) — the
two require opposite responses and conflating them leads to the wrong intervention.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR13: FREEMIUM PRODUCT — CONVERSION BELOW 2% AFTER 12 MONTHS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
A freemium product that has been live for 12 or more months has a free-to-paid
conversion rate below 2% — meaning fewer than 1 in 50 free users become paying
customers. Detectable when total registered or free user count and total paying
customer count are both disclosed or calculable.

BENCHMARK:
Free-to-paid conversion rates by model:
B2B freemium (SMB target): STRONG >8% | ACCEPTABLE 3–8% | CONCERN 2–3% | FAIL <2%
Consumer freemium: STRONG >5% | ACCEPTABLE 2–5% | CONCERN 1–2% | FAIL <1%
A company reporting 50,000 users and 500 paying customers has a 1% conversion rate
regardless of what it claims about its "engaged user base."

WHAT IT MEANS:
A sub-2% conversion rate in a B2B freemium product after 12 months means one of
three things: (1) the free tier is too generous — users receive full or near-full
value from the free tier and have no material incentive to upgrade; (2) the paid
tier upgrade triggers do not align with genuine user needs — users hit the paywall
on features they could live without, and simply stop using those features rather
than paying; (3) the free users are not the target buyer profile — the product is
attracting users who would never pay (students, individuals, hobby users) while
the intended enterprise or SMB buyer is not adopting the free tier at all.
The consequence: the company is running an infrastructure, support, and customer
success operation for thousands of free users who are generating no revenue while
the paid tier revenue is insufficient to cover those costs.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Conversion rate has been below 2% for more than 12 months AND free user base is
above 5,000 (so the rate is statistically meaningful) AND the company has not
made a structural change to the free/paid tier boundary — the model has been given
sufficient time to prove itself and has not.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The company has deliberately maintained a generous free tier as a category-building
strategy during an early market development phase, and has a documented plan to adjust
the free/paid boundary with a specific timeline.
(b) The free tier is a consumer acquisition channel for a B2B product — free individual
users at a company organically become a paid enterprise contract through bottom-up
adoption. Conversion from individual free user to enterprise contract is tracked
separately and is above 5%.
(c) The conversion rate has been improving quarter-on-quarter even if still below
2% — a trend from 0.8% to 1.5% over 6 quarters indicates the model is working
but still developing.

VERIFICATION:
Request monthly free user additions, paying customer additions, and the conversion
rate calculation for the last 6 months. Ask: "What does a free user who converts
to paid look like — what is the trigger? And what is the most common reason free
users do not convert?" The trigger story reveals whether there is a genuine upgrade
event or whether conversion is incidental.

PATTERN LESSON:
When a freemium product has a conversion rate below 2% after 12 months, test whether
the free tier is providing full value at zero cost — if free users can accomplish
the primary job-to-be-done without paying, the conversion problem is structural
and requires a tier redesign, not a marketing improvement.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR14: REFERENCE CUSTOMERS DECLINING TO SPEAK PUBLICLY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
The company has 5 or more paying customers and cannot produce a single customer
who will: (a) take a 20-minute reference call with an investor without a structured
NDA; or (b) be named on the company's website or in a case study with their own
name and company attached. All references offered are anonymous, under strict NDA,
or unavailable for timing or confidentiality reasons. Note: this flag also appears
as Team Flag T15 — it is included in both categories because it signals both a team
quality issue (champion relationships are not strong enough to generate advocacy)
and a traction quality issue (customers are not receiving enough value to advocate).

WHAT IT MEANS:
A paying customer who declines to be a public reference is sending a clear signal:
the product or relationship is not valuable enough to the customer to justify the
small effort of advocacy. There are three legitimate exceptions — regulated industries
(financial services, healthcare, government) where disclosure of technology vendors
is restricted; extremely early relationships where the customer has not yet seen
enough value to advocate; or enterprise companies with strict marketing approval
processes that slow, not prevent, reference cases. Beyond these exceptions, customer
reference unwillingness is the most direct possible signal that product-market fit
is not confirmed at the level the submission implies. A customer who loves a product
and uses it actively is almost always willing to say so — the advocacy reluctance
is inversely correlated with the depth of the value delivered.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The company has more than 10 paying customers AND none are willing to be named
publicly AND the company does not operate in a sector where disclosure is structurally
restricted AND the company is presenting customer satisfaction as strong (high NPS,
positive anecdotes) — the disconnect between claimed satisfaction and absence of
advocacy is a material credibility signal.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The company operates in a sector where vendor disclosure is structurally restricted
— NHS trusts and financial services firms routinely restrict technology vendor naming
in press — and the company can provide reference calls under NDA with senior decision-makers.
(b) The company is very early (fewer than 5 customers) and relationships are new —
customers have not yet had a 12-month experience to reflect on.
(c) The company has 1 or more public case studies but the rest of the base prefers
anonymity — the flag triggers when zero customers will be publicly named, not when
the majority declines.

VERIFICATION:
Request 3 reference customers — senior decision-makers willing to take a 20-minute
call without a structured NDA — as a standard part of screening. If the company
cannot provide 3 within 5 business days after having 10+ customers, ask: "Can you
share why your customers prefer to remain anonymous? Is this sector-specific or
have specific customers expressed concerns about the reference?" The answer either
identifies a legitimate structural reason or reveals a relationship quality issue.

PATTERN LESSON:
When reference customers decline to speak publicly with more than 5 paying customers
in the base, ask directly whether the reluctance is sector-structural or relationship-based
— the distinction determines whether it is a constraint to be managed or a product
satisfaction signal to be investigated.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG TR15: ARR GROWTH DRIVEN BY PRICE INCREASES NOT VOLUME
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Traction
SEVERITY: MEDIUM

SIGNAL:
ARR growth in the reported period is driven primarily by price increases applied to
existing customers — not by new customer acquisition, not by genuine seat or usage
expansion — and the underlying volume growth (new logos, new seats, new use cases)
is flat or declining. Detectable when: the company reports strong NRR but flat or
declining new logo count; the submission describes a pricing action applied in the
period that accounts for a significant portion of the ARR increase; or average ACV
per customer has increased while customer count has not.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A price increase that customers accept and renew at is positive evidence of pricing
power. But when price increases mask stagnant volume, three problems emerge:
(1) The price increase is a one-time event — it cannot be repeated in the next period
at the same magnitude without risking churn. The ARR growth contribution from pricing
is non-recurring even if the revenue is contractually recurring.
(2) Volume stagnation signals that the product is not attracting enough new buyers
at the new price point — the higher price may be correct for current customers but
is pricing out new customers who would have converted at the prior price.
(3) Existing customers who accepted the increase once may resist a second increase
at renewal — the customer who paid £80K instead of £60K this year may not accept
£100K next year, and the loss of one large account at renewal will be larger
than the gain from the price increase.
ARR growth driven entirely by price is not product-led growth — it is yield management,
and it is not indefinitely sustainable as the primary growth lever.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
More than 70% of net new ARR in the last 12 months came from price increases on
existing customers AND new logo count has been flat or declining for 2 or more
consecutive quarters AND the company's financial model projects continued ARR growth
at the same rate — implying either continued price increases or new volume that
the current data does not support.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The price increase reflects genuine value increase — a major product improvement,
a new module with tangible ROI — rather than a pure yield extraction. Customers
who receive more value paying more is not a red flag; it is appropriate pricing.
(b) Price increases represent less than 30% of net new ARR and volume growth is
the primary driver — the pricing action is additive, not substitutive.
(c) The company is at an early stage correcting a systematic under-pricing from
the initial go-to-market — a one-time correction that moves customers from £10K
to £20K ACV is not a volume substitution, it is a pricing model correction.

VERIFICATION:
Request net new ARR broken into components: new logos × new logo ACV, existing
customer expansions (seat/usage), and pricing adjustments on existing contracts.
Ask: "Of your ARR growth in the last 12 months, how much came from new customers
versus existing customers expanding their usage versus price increases?" The breakdown
reveals whether growth is volume-led, expansion-led, or yield-led.

PATTERN LESSON:
When new logo count is flat while ARR is growing, decompose the ARR growth into
new logos, seat and usage expansion, and price increases — if more than 50% of net
new ARR is from price increases, the growth story is not a volume story, and the
sustainability of that growth depends on whether the company can repeat the pricing
action or convert price acceptance into genuine volume growth.
