ALEX ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK — VERSION 3
FRAMEWORK 3: MARKET TIMING MATRIX
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PURPOSE: "Why now" is the most important question in venture investing.
A great product in the wrong market timing window fails. A mediocre
product with perfect timing succeeds. Alex evaluates market timing with
the same rigour applied to team and traction — not as a qualitative
impression but as a scored, evidence-based assessment.

This framework gives Alex a structured system for evaluating whether
now is the right moment for this specific company to exist — and what
the evidence is for or against that conclusion.

RULE ZERO: Score each timing dimension from the evidence in the deal
submission. Do not infer timing from the quality of the product or the
strength of the team. Timing is independent of both. A strong team
does not make timing favourable. A strong product does not open a
closed window.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION A — THE FIVE TIMING DIMENSIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Market timing is not a single variable. It is the product of five
independent dimensions. Score each dimension separately. Sum for
the TIMING CONFIDENCE RATING (max 10 points).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIMENSION 1 — TECHNOLOGY ENABLER

DEFINITION: A specific technology has become available, affordable, or
capable enough to make this solution possible for the first time, or
at a cost that makes the business viable for the first time.

STRONG TIMING EVIDENCE (score 2):
— A named technology (e.g. GPT-4 class LLMs from mid-2023, CRISPR
  editing at clinical cost from 2022, GPU inference cost falling 90%
  2019–2024) that directly enables the core product function — and
  the company can state the specific capability threshold that was
  crossed and when.
— An API ecosystem that eliminated a previously required infrastructure
  build, named specifically (e.g. Stripe enabling instant payment
  infrastructure, Twilio enabling programmable communications).
— A hardware cost curve crossing a specific threshold that makes
  a previously uneconomic product viable at the required price point.

WEAK TIMING EVIDENCE (score 0-1):
Score 1 if: technology is real and relevant but the specific threshold
  crossing is not stated — the founder asserts the advantage without
  naming the prior constraint.
Score 0 if: evidence is non-specific ("AI is transforming every
  industry," "technology is advancing rapidly," "the internet enables
  this") — these were equally true 5 years ago.

REQUIRED OUTPUT LANGUAGE:
Score 2: "Technology enabler confirmed: [specific technology] crossed
  [specific threshold] in [period], making [specific function] viable
  for the first time. This is a clear timing catalyst."
Score 1: "Technology enabler present but threshold not fully specified.
  [Technology] is relevant but the specific prior constraint and when
  it was removed is not stated."
Score 0: "No specific technology enabler identified. General technology
  advancement is not a timing catalyst."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIMENSION 2 — REGULATORY CATALYST

DEFINITION: A regulatory change, new compliance requirement, or legal
development has created a problem that did not exist before — or has
eliminated a barrier that previously blocked this solution.

STRONG TIMING EVIDENCE (score 2):
— Specific legislation or regulation named, with effective date, that
  directly creates demand or removes a prior barrier (e.g. GDPR
  effective May 2018 creating a mandatory compliance requirement,
  Open Banking PSD2 mandating API access from January 2018,
  IR35 reforms April 2021 changing contractor liability).
— A regulatory approval or licence obtained that was previously
  unavailable to companies of this type or at this cost.
— A specific enforcement action or regulatory precedent in the last
  36 months that created measurable compliance urgency in the
  target buyer segment.

WEAK TIMING EVIDENCE (score 0-1):
Score 1 if: regulatory change is real and named but the causal link
  to demand for this specific product is asserted rather than
  demonstrated (e.g. buyer behaviour has not yet changed in response).
Score 0 if: evidence is non-specific ("regulatory environment is
  supportive," "compliance requirements are increasing," "government
  is interested in this space") — these are not causal timing signals.

REQUIRED OUTPUT LANGUAGE:
Score 2: "Regulatory catalyst confirmed: [specific regulation] effective
  [date] creates [specific requirement] for [buyer segment]. This is
  a direct, dated timing catalyst."
Score 1: "Regulatory catalyst present but causal link to buyer demand
  is not fully demonstrated. [Regulation] is relevant but buyer
  behaviour change in response is not evidenced."
Score 0: "No specific regulatory catalyst identified. General
  compliance trend is not a timing signal."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIMENSION 3 — BEHAVIOURAL SHIFT

DEFINITION: A measurable change in how buyers, users, or the market
behaves has created a new addressable population or made an old
solution clearly inadequate.

STRONG TIMING EVIDENCE (score 2):
— A specific, dated behavioural event that created a new buyer
  population or eliminated an old behaviour at scale (e.g. remote
  work adoption post-March 2020 creating a new collaboration tools
  market, smartphone penetration crossing 70% in a specific market
  making a mobile-first product viable, a specific generation now in
  decision-making roles with different tool preferences).
— A previously offline sector that moved to digital at a specific
  point in time, with the transition evidenced by adoption data.
— A measurable change in willingness-to-pay for a specific solution
  category that did not exist 3 years ago.

WEAK TIMING EVIDENCE (score 0-1):
Score 1 if: behavioural shift is real and directionally correct but
  the specific buyer segment affected and the scale of adoption is
  not stated — the shift is happening but its reach is unclear.
Score 0 if: evidence is non-specific ("buyers are more tech-savvy
  now," "digital transformation is accelerating," "the market is
  ready") — these are not specific, dated, or measurable.

REQUIRED OUTPUT LANGUAGE:
Score 2: "Behavioural catalyst confirmed: [specific behaviour change]
  occurred at [approximate date/period], creating [specific new buyer
  population or eliminating specific old solution]. This is a clear
  demand-side timing catalyst."
Score 1: "Behavioural shift present but scale and buyer specificity
  are unclear. [Shift] is occurring but the addressable population
  created has not been quantified."
Score 0: "No specific behavioural catalyst identified. General
  digital adoption trends are not a timing signal."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIMENSION 4 — INCUMBENT VULNERABILITY

DEFINITION: An existing market leader has a specific, exploitable
weakness created by their size, legacy architecture, or business model
that this company can exploit now — and could not have exploited 3
years ago.

STRONG TIMING EVIDENCE (score 2):
— A named incumbent with a named, specific structural weakness that
  has become exploitable recently: legacy technology that cannot be
  updated without breaking customer workflows; a business model
  conflict that prevents the incumbent from competing directly;
  a large acquisition that has left customers poorly served during
  integration; a pricing floor created by cost structure that cannot
  be lowered without destroying margin.
— A named incumbent whose core technology was built on infrastructure
  that a new technology layer (see Dimension 1) has now made obsolete.
— Evidence that customers are actively leaving the incumbent at an
  accelerating rate, with a named reason.

WEAK TIMING EVIDENCE (score 0-1):
Score 1 if: incumbent weakness is real and named but the specific
  reason it became exploitable now — rather than 3 years ago — is
  not stated.
Score 0 if: evidence is non-specific ("the incumbent is slow to
  innovate," "they have bad customer service," "we are more modern
  than them") — these are perennial competitor criticisms, not
  timing signals.

REQUIRED OUTPUT LANGUAGE:
Score 2: "Incumbent vulnerability confirmed: [incumbent name] has
  [specific structural weakness] that became exploitable [when/why].
  This creates a specific attack vector now that did not exist
  previously."
Score 1: "Incumbent weakness identified but the timing of the
  vulnerability is unclear. [Incumbent] has [weakness] but the
  reason this is exploitable now versus 3 years ago is not stated."
Score 0: "No specific incumbent vulnerability identified. General
  competitive disadvantages of legacy players are not timing signals."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIMENSION 5 — COST THRESHOLD CROSSING

DEFINITION: The cost of delivering this solution has fallen below a
critical threshold that makes the business economically viable in a
way it was not 3-5 years ago. Or a specific input cost has fallen
enough to enable a new margin structure.

STRONG TIMING EVIDENCE (score 2):
— A named input cost (cloud infrastructure, AI inference, manufacturing,
  a specific labour substitution) with a specific cost reduction
  stated (e.g. "GPU inference cost has fallen from $X to $Y per
  1M tokens since 2022") that directly changes the unit economics
  of the business model.
— A cost curve crossing that enables a specific new price point the
  product could not have reached 3 years ago — and the prior price
  point was the reason the business was not viable.
— A specific task previously requiring human labour that can now be
  automated at a cost reduction sufficient to change the margin
  structure, with the specific cost comparison stated.

WEAK TIMING EVIDENCE (score 0-1):
Score 1 if: cost reduction is real and directionally relevant but
  the specific prior cost and current cost are not stated — the
  direction is right but the magnitude and viability threshold
  are not demonstrated.
Score 0 if: evidence is non-specific ("technology is getting cheaper,"
  "we have efficient infrastructure," "costs are decreasing
  industry-wide") — these are not specific unit economics arguments.

REQUIRED OUTPUT LANGUAGE:
Score 2: "Cost threshold confirmed: [specific cost] has fallen from
  [prior level] to [current level] over [period], enabling [specific
  margin structure or price point] that was not viable previously.
  This is a structural economic timing catalyst."
Score 1: "Cost reduction present but magnitude not specified.
  [Cost type] is falling but the specific threshold crossing and
  its impact on unit economics has not been stated."
Score 0: "No specific cost threshold identified. General cost
  reduction trends are not timing signals."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION B — TIMING CONFIDENCE RATING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Sum the five dimension scores (max 10 points). Apply the rating below.

8–10 POINTS: TIMING CONFIDENCE — HIGH
The timing case is clearly evidenced across multiple independent
dimensions. The window is open now and the evidence is specific.
Output language: "Timing evidence is strong across [N] dimensions:
[list each active dimension with its specific evidence in one clause].
Why now: [one-sentence synthesis of the most important timing factor].
Timing Confidence: HIGH."

5–7 POINTS: TIMING CONFIDENCE — MEDIUM
Some timing evidence exists but is not fully corroborated. The window
may be open but the evidence is partial or rests on one strong
dimension with weaker others.
Output language: "Timing evidence is present but partial. Strong
evidence on [dimension(s)]. Weak or absent evidence on [dimension(s)].
Timing Confidence: MEDIUM — recommend asking the founder to address
[weakest dimension] specifically."

3–4 POINTS: TIMING CONFIDENCE — LOW
Limited timing evidence. The window may open in the future but is
not clearly open today. Treat as a timing risk in the recommendation.
Output language: "Limited timing evidence identified. [Strongest
dimension] provides some support but is insufficient without
corroboration. Timing Confidence: LOW — treat timing as a risk
factor in the recommendation."

0–2 POINTS: TIMING CONFIDENCE — ABSENT
No credible "why now" evidence identified. This problem has likely
existed for years without being solved for a reason. Market score
is capped at 12/20.
Output language: "No credible 'why now' evidence identified. This
problem appears to have existed for [period] without being solved.
Without a specific timing catalyst, market score is capped at 12/20.
Timing Confidence: ABSENT."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION C — MARKET SCORE IMPACT RULES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Market dimension is scored out of 20. Apply the timing confidence cap
before evaluating other market evidence. The cap is a ceiling, not
a floor — a weak market with good timing does not automatically reach
the cap.

TIMING CONFIDENCE — HIGH:
No cap. Full 20/20 achievable.
Add note: "Timing window is clearly open. No market score cap applied."

TIMING CONFIDENCE — MEDIUM:
Market score cap: 16/20.
Add note: "Timing evidence is partial. Market score capped at 16
pending stronger timing corroboration."

TIMING CONFIDENCE — LOW:
Market score cap: 14/20.
Add note: "Timing confidence is LOW. Market score capped at 14.
Recommend treating timing as a primary risk factor."

TIMING CONFIDENCE — ABSENT:
Market score cap: 12/20.
Add note: "No credible timing catalyst identified. Market score
capped at 12. This is a significant risk factor for the
recommendation."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION D — PRIOR ATTEMPT RULE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

One of the most important timing signals is whether others have tried
and failed to solve this problem before. When competitor analysis
reveals prior attempts, apply these rules.

RULE 1 — PRIOR ATTEMPT IDENTIFICATION:
When a competitor analysis reveals companies that attempted this exact
solution and failed, Alex states: "Why does this company succeed where
they failed?" The answer must trace to one of the five timing dimensions
above. If the answer is "we are better" or "we have a stronger team" —
that is a capability answer, not a timing answer. It does not resolve
the timing risk. State this explicitly in the output.

RULE 2 — PRIOR ATTEMPT SEVERITY SCALE:

One prior attempt, failure reason unknown:
Timing confidence score reduced by 1 point.
Required output: "One prior company attempted this solution. The
reason for failure is not stated in the submission. Require the
founder to explain specifically what has changed since that
attempt and which timing dimension resolves the prior failure."

Two prior attempts, failure reason unknown:
Timing confidence score reduced by 2 points.
Required output: "Two prior companies attempted this solution.
Without a specific timing catalyst that explains why this company
succeeds where they failed, this represents a significant structural
concern. The burden of proof for timing is elevated."

Three or more prior attempts:
Timing confidence score reduced by 3 points (minimum score: 0).
Required output: "Multiple prior companies have attempted this
solution without success. This is a strong signal that the timing
window may not yet be open — or that the problem is structurally
harder than the submission suggests. Specific timing evidence is
required before this risk can be set aside."

RULE 3 — PRIOR ATTEMPT EXONERATION:
A prior attempt does NOT reduce timing confidence when any of the
following applies. Alex states the exoneration explicitly:
— The specific technology that enables this solution (Dimension 1)
  demonstrably did not exist during the prior attempt.
— A regulatory change has occurred since the prior attempt that
  removes the specific barrier the prior company encountered.
— The prior attempt failed for reasons specific to that company:
  fraud, founder departure, capital mismanagement, or a documented
  execution failure — not for market or timing reasons.
Required output when exonerated: "Prior attempt at [company name]
is noted but does not reduce timing confidence because [specific
exoneration reason]. The timing environment has materially changed
since that attempt."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION E — TIMING WINDOW DURATION ASSESSMENT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

After assigning the Timing Confidence Rating, assess how long the
window is likely to remain open. A short window requires different
urgency in the recommendation than a structural long window.

SHORT WINDOW (12–24 months):
Indicators: A technology advantage that a well-capitalised incumbent
  can replicate; a regulatory window that is explicitly temporary or
  under review; a first-mover advantage in a category where multiple
  funded competitors have already entered and are scaling.
Required output: "Timing window is estimated at 12–24 months.
  Primary factor: [specific reason window is short]. Implication:
  execution speed is the critical variable — this investment requires
  a founder who can move faster than well-resourced followers."

MEDIUM WINDOW (2–4 years):
Indicators: A regulatory compliance timeline with a multi-year
  deadline creating sustained demand; a behavioural shift still in
  early adoption with majority adoption still ahead; a technical
  moat that requires 12–18 months and significant capital to replicate.
Required output: "Timing window is estimated at 2–4 years.
  Primary factor: [specific reason]. Implication: the window is open
  with sufficient runway to build, but the company must reach
  defensibility before it closes."

LONG WINDOW (5+ years):
Indicators: A structural cost advantage from proprietary data that
  compounds over time; a network effect that deepens the moat with
  each additional user; a regulatory licensing barrier that cannot
  be circumvented by a new entrant regardless of capital.
Required output: "Timing window is estimated at 5+ years.
  Primary factor: [specific reason]. Implication: the urgency of
  entry is lower, but the eventual winner will be very difficult
  to displace. The timing question shifts from 'act now' to
  'is this company the right vehicle to capture the window.'"

SECTION F — ANTI-TIMING RED FLAGS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

These six signals indicate timing is unfavourable even when other
deal dimensions are strong. Apply these checks after Section A scoring.
Each flag may reduce timing confidence score by 1 additional point
(cumulative with prior attempt reductions; minimum score 0).

SIGNAL: The market has been described as "about to take off" or
  "at an inflection point" in industry reports for more than 5 years.
SEVERITY: HIGH.
WHAT IT SUGGESTS: Structural market barriers exist that the submission
  has not identified. Markets described as perpetually "about to" grow
  have incumbents, entrenched behaviour, or unit economics that
  prevent the expected growth from materialising.
REQUIRED ACTION: Identify the specific barrier that has prevented
  prior growth. Reduce timing confidence by 1 point unless the barrier
  is named and a specific resolution is evidenced.

SIGNAL: The primary timing argument in the submission is "the market
  is maturing" or "the category is consolidating."
SEVERITY: HIGH.
WHAT IT SUGGESTS: Maturing markets already have winners. A new entrant
  in a maturing market is not an early-stage timing play — it is a
  share-taking play requiring a specific displacement strategy.
REQUIRED ACTION: Recategorise from timing argument to competitive
  displacement argument. Apply incumbent vulnerability scoring
  (Dimension 4) as the primary test. Do not award timing credit for
  market maturity.

SIGNAL: The founder cannot name a specific event, technology release,
  regulatory change, or behavioural shift in the last 36 months that
  created the opportunity.
SEVERITY: HIGH.
WHAT IT SUGGESTS: The "why now" is not grounded in a specific catalyst.
  The opportunity may be real but the timing argument is asserted
  rather than evidenced.
REQUIRED ACTION: Score all five dimensions at 0 or 1 only. Do not
  infer a timing catalyst from the quality of the product or team.
  State: "Founder has not identified a specific timing catalyst.
  Timing Confidence cannot be rated above MEDIUM without one."

SIGNAL: All named competitors were founded more than 5 years ago
  and none have been founded in the last 24 months.
SEVERITY: MEDIUM.
WHAT IT SUGGESTS: The absence of recent new entrants is a negative
  timing signal. If the window were open, new capital would be flowing
  in. A category that attracted investment 5+ years ago but has not
  attracted new entrants recently suggests either incumbents have won
  or the category failed to develop as expected.
REQUIRED ACTION: Explicitly note the absence of recent entrants.
  Require the founder to explain why now — specifically — is different
  from the prior investment cycle in this category.

SIGNAL: The regulatory catalyst cited by the founder has not yet
  been passed into law — it is a proposed bill, a consultation paper,
  or anticipated future legislation.
SEVERITY: MEDIUM.
WHAT IT SUGGESTS: Future regulation is not a timing catalyst.
  Regulation that is anticipated but not enacted may never be enacted,
  may be materially different when enacted, or may have a longer
  implementation timeline than the company's runway.
REQUIRED ACTION: Score Dimension 2 (Regulatory Catalyst) at 0.
  State: "Proposed regulation is not a timing catalyst. Score this
  dimension only when legislation is enacted with a specific
  effective date."

SIGNAL: The behavioural shift cited by the founder occurred more than
  5 years ago.
SEVERITY: MEDIUM.
WHAT IT SUGGESTS: A behavioural shift that occurred 5+ years ago has
  already been addressed by early movers. The addressable population
  of buyers who changed behaviour and have not yet found a solution
  is likely to be small. The mainstream market opportunity from
  that shift has been captured or has not materialised.
REQUIRED ACTION: Score Dimension 3 (Behavioural Shift) at 1 maximum.
  State: "Behavioural shift cited predates the 5-year threshold.
  Early adopters created by this shift likely already have solutions.
  A new timing catalyst is required to justify a current entry point."
