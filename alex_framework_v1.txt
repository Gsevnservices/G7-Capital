ALEX ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK — VERSION 1
FRAMEWORK 1: BUSINESS MODEL DECOMPOSITION AND UNIT ECONOMICS INFERENCE ENGINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PURPOSE: When deal materials contain incomplete financial data, Alex does
not write "data not provided." Alex infers missing metrics from available
signals using the rules below, states his confidence level, and flags
when an inference materially affects the recommendation.

RULE ZERO: Every inference must be traceable to a specific signal in the
deal materials or a model-type benchmark. Never infer from narrative alone.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION A — BUSINESS MODEL IDENTIFICATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Identify model type from deal description before applying any inference
rule. If two types are plausible, apply Section F hybrid rules first.

MODEL TYPE 1 — PURE SAAS
Identification signals: subscription pricing only, no implementation
revenue line, ARR or MRR reported directly.
Primary revenue driver: recurring license fee per seat or per account.
Key unit economics: ARR, NRR, gross margin, CAC, LTV:CAC, logo churn.
Expected gross margin: 70–85%. Below 65% triggers SaaS + Services check.
Valuation framework: ARR multiple. 8–12x for <50% YoY growth. 12–20x
for >80% YoY growth with NRR >110%. Above 20x requires category
leadership evidence.

MODEL TYPE 2 — SAAS + SERVICES
Identification signals: professional services, implementation, or customer
success revenue listed separately. Services >15% of total revenue.
Primary revenue driver: subscription license (apply check: if services
>40% of revenue, reclassify as Professional Services primary).
Key unit economics: blended gross margin, services attach rate, ARR
as % of total revenue, services gross margin separate from software GM.
Expected gross margin: 55–72% blended. Software layer alone: 70–82%.
Valuation framework: discounted ARR multiple. Apply 70–80% of Pure SaaS
comparable multiple to reflect services drag.

MODEL TYPE 3 — MARKETPLACE
Identification signals: take rate language, GMV reported, two-sided
network described, company does not own inventory.
Primary revenue driver: GMV × take rate (net revenue).
Key unit economics: GMV, take rate, gross merchandise retention, supply
and demand concentration, liquidity (transactions per active listing).
Expected gross margin: 60–80% of net revenue. If marketplace also
holds inventory or handles fulfillment, reduce to 35–55%.
Valuation framework: GMV multiple (0.5–3x) or net revenue multiple
(5–15x depending on take rate and growth). Higher take rate = higher
revenue multiple.

MODEL TYPE 4 — TRANSACTION / PAYMENTS
Identification signals: volume-based pricing in basis points or flat
fee per transaction, TPV (total payment volume) reported, interchange
or payment processing language.
Primary revenue driver: TPV × net revenue rate.
Key unit economics: TPV, net revenue rate (after interchange), payment
gross margin, take rate net of processing costs.
Expected gross margin: 40–65%. Interchange-heavy models: 40–55%.
Software-augmented payment models: 55–70%.
Valuation framework: revenue multiple (3–8x) based on net revenue,
not gross payment volume. Never apply ARR multiples.

MODEL TYPE 5 — USAGE-BASED / CONSUMPTION
Identification signals: pricing per API call, per GB processed, per
message sent, per active user above a threshold. No fixed contract value.
Primary revenue driver: consumption volume × unit price.
Key unit economics: dollar-based NRR (critical — expansion is the core
thesis), revenue predictability, committed vs. variable revenue ratio.
Expected gross margin: 60–80%. Infrastructure-intensive models: 55–70%.
Valuation framework: ARR multiple discounted 10–20% vs. Pure SaaS
comparable to reflect revenue unpredictability. If committed ARR >60%
of total, apply full SaaS multiple.

MODEL TYPE 6 — HARDWARE + SOFTWARE
Identification signals: physical device sold or leased plus recurring
software or service fee. Device mentioned in product description.
Primary revenue driver: determine by revenue split. If software >60%
of revenue, apply SaaS primary framework. If hardware >60%, apply
hardware framework.
Key unit economics: hardware gross margin (often 10–30%), software
attach rate, blended LTV, hardware payback.
Expected gross margin: 30–55% blended. Software layer alone: 65–80%.
Hardware alone: 10–35%.
Valuation framework: blended. Weight SaaS multiple by software revenue
%, hardware multiple (1–3x revenue) by hardware revenue %. If company
is subsidising hardware with software gross margin, flag as risk.

MODEL TYPE 7 — E-COMMERCE / DTC
Identification signals: physical product sold direct to consumer,
SKU count mentioned, COGS includes manufactured goods, customer repeat
purchase language.
Primary revenue driver: revenue × product gross margin.
Key unit economics: AOV (average order value), repeat purchase rate,
contribution margin (GM minus CAC), LTV:CAC.
Expected gross margin: 30–60%. Commodity products: 30–40%. Premium or
proprietary: 45–65%. If above 65%, verify no services component.
Valuation framework: revenue multiple (0.5–2.5x) or EBITDA multiple
(8–15x) if profitable. Never apply ARR multiples to DTC businesses.

MODEL TYPE 8 — PROFESSIONAL SERVICES / CONSULTING
Identification signals: billable hours, project-based fees, retainer
language, headcount scales linearly with revenue, utilisation rate mentioned.
Primary revenue driver: billable hours × hourly rate, or project value.
Key unit economics: utilisation rate (target: 70–80%), billing rate,
revenue per head, headcount growth vs. revenue growth ratio.
Expected gross margin: 25–42%. If above 45%, check for productised
services or IP component that may warrant reclassification.
Valuation framework: EBITDA multiple (5–10x) or revenue multiple
(0.5–1.5x). Never apply SaaS or ARR multiples. Growth premium applies
only if utilisation is expanding, not just headcount.

MODEL TYPE 9 — MEDIA / CONTENT
Identification signals: advertising revenue (CPM, CPC), subscriber-based
content access, audience size reported, content production costs as COGS.
Sub-type A (Advertising): revenue = impressions × CPM.
Sub-type B (Subscription): revenue = subscribers × ARPU.
Key unit economics: Sub-A: CPM, RPM, audience retention. Sub-B: subscriber
NRR, content cost per subscriber, churn.
Expected gross margin: Sub-A: 40–65% (content cost-dependent). Sub-B:
50–75% at scale. Below 35% in either type indicates unsustainable content spend.
Valuation framework: EBITDA multiple (8–15x) for mature media.
Revenue multiple (2–6x) for high-growth audience businesses. Subscriber
LTV multiple for subscription businesses.

MODEL TYPE 10 — DEEP TECH / IP LICENSING
Identification signals: licensing language, royalty structure, patent
portfolio mentioned, B2B2C or partner-dependent distribution, milestone
payments, government or defence contracts.
Primary revenue driver: license fee, royalty per unit, or milestone.
Key unit economics: R&D spend as % of revenue, time to commercialisation,
licensing economics per unit, customer concentration in licensor base.
Expected gross margin: Pure IP licensing: 85–97%. If manufactured product
incorporating IP: 20–45%. Technology transfer with ongoing support: 55–75%.
Valuation framework: DCF (primary, due to milestone structure) or comparable
licensing transaction multiples. Never apply SaaS ARR multiples unless
the IP is delivered as software-as-a-service.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION B — INFERENCE RULES BY MODEL TYPE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

─── B1. GROSS MARGIN INFERENCE ───

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Pure SaaS (Type 1):
INFER 72–80% based on category benchmark for cloud-delivered software.
EXCEPTION: if on-premise deployment mentioned, adjust to 62–74%.
EXCEPTION: if company reports significant DevOps or infrastructure cost
in materials, adjust to 65–75%.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM (no company-specific signal available).
STATE IN OUTPUT: "Gross margin not disclosed. Inferring 72–80% based
on Pure SaaS category benchmark. Confidence: MEDIUM."

IF gross margin not provided AND model is SaaS + Services (Type 2):
INFER blended 58–68%. Calculate as: (software GM 75%) × (ARR % of
revenue) + (services GM 28%) × (services % of revenue).
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM if revenue split known; LOW if revenue split unknown.
STATE IN OUTPUT: "Gross margin not disclosed. Blended margin inferred
at [X]% based on [ARR %] subscription / [services %] services split.
Confidence: [level]."

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Marketplace (Type 3):
INFER 65–75% of net revenue (post-payment processing).
EXCEPTION: if marketplace handles fulfillment or logistics, adjust to 35–50%.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Transaction/Payments (Type 4):
Identify sub-category before inferring:
— Consumer payments (e.g. Stripe, PayPal-type consumer): INFER 40–52%.
  Key signal: high volume, low-margin interchange, consumer card processing.
— B2B payments / infrastructure (e.g. embedded finance, API-based): INFER 45–58%.
  Key signal: B2B language, infrastructure positioning, API-first product.
— Merchant of Record (e.g. Paddle-type MoR): INFER 42–55%.
  Key signal: company takes legal responsibility for transaction, handles
  tax compliance, currency conversion described as core feature.
If sub-category is ambiguous: apply the Consumer payments range (40–52%)
as the conservative default.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Usage-Based (Type 5):
INFER 62–75%. If AI/ML inference costs mentioned, adjust to 55–68%.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Hardware + Software (Type 6):
Step 1 — Infer hardware GM: 15–30% unless specific hardware mentioned.
Step 2 — Infer software GM: 70–80%.
Step 3 — Calculate blended using revenue split if available.
If revenue split not available, assume 50/50: blended = 42–55%.
CONFIDENCE: LOW (two inference layers required).

IF gross margin not provided AND model is E-commerce/DTC (Type 7):
INFER 38–52%. If premium brand positioning stated, upper end (48–60%).
If commodity/price-competitive positioning, lower end (30–42%).
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Professional Services (Type 8):
INFER 30–38% based on services business benchmark.
EXCEPTION: if offshore delivery model stated, adjust to 38–48%.
CONFIDENCE: HIGH (narrow range; model type constrains significantly).

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Media/Content (Type 9):
Sub-A (Advertising): INFER 45–60%.
Sub-B (Subscription): INFER 52–68% at current scale; note margin expands
with scale as content costs amortise.
CONFIDENCE: LOW (content cost structure highly variable).

IF gross margin not provided AND model is Deep Tech/IP (Type 10):
INFER based on delivery mechanism:
— Pure licensing: 88–96%.
— Licensed + manufactured: 25–42%.
— SaaS delivery of IP: 70–82%.
CONFIDENCE: HIGH for pure licensing; MEDIUM for mixed delivery.

─── B2. NRR INFERENCE ───

IF NRR not provided AND model is Pure SaaS (Type 1):
INFER based on customer segment:
— Enterprise (ACV >$50K): 105–125%.
— Mid-Market (ACV $10K–$50K): 100–115%.
— SMB (ACV <$10K): 88–105%.
Key signal: if customer count >500 and ACV <$5K, infer SMB range.
EXCEPTION: if product described as mission-critical workflow (ERP,
payroll, compliance), add 5–8 points to upper bound.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM if segment identifiable; LOW if not.
STATE IN OUTPUT: "NRR not disclosed. Inferring [X]% for [segment]
SaaS based on category benchmark and [signal]. Confidence: [level]."

IF NRR not provided AND model is Usage-Based (Type 5):
INFER 115–145% based on natural expansion mechanics.
EXCEPTION: if new customer growth is the primary growth driver (PLG),
infer 105–125% (expansion is lower because land is small).
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF NRR not provided AND model is Marketplace (Type 3):
INFER GMV retention of 110–140% for supply side (merchants expand).
Note: NRR concept applies to supply-side GMV, not traditional ARR churn.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF NRR not provided AND model is Transaction/Payments (Type 4):
INFER 108–130% based on same-merchant volume expansion.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF NRR not provided AND model is SaaS + Services (Type 2):
INFER 95–118%. Services component expands with company growth but may
churn differently than software licenses.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF NRR not provided AND model is Professional Services (Type 8):
INFER 80–98%. Retainer-based models: 88–102%. Project-based: 75–92%.
CONFIDENCE: LOW (highly relationship-dependent).

IF NRR not provided AND model is E-commerce (Type 7):
NRR metric is not applicable. Use cohort retention instead.
INFER 12-month cohort revenue retention: 40–70% (highly brand-dependent).
STATE IN OUTPUT: "NRR not applicable for DTC model. Inferring 12-month
cohort retention of [X]% based on [brand tier/category]. Confidence: LOW."

IF NRR not provided AND model is Hardware + Software (Type 6):
NRR is driven by software subscription renewal, not hardware repurchase.
Key signal: is hardware sold as one-time purchase or as a subscription?
— One-time hardware purchase: NRR measures software renewal only.
  Apply SMB SaaS benchmarks to the software component: INFER 88–105%.
— Hardware subscription (device-as-a-service): INFER 95–108%.
  Expansion comes from fleet growth, not seat expansion.
CONFIDENCE: LOW — hardware revenue distorts the blended metric
significantly and the split is rarely disclosed.
STATE IN OUTPUT: "NRR inferred at [X]% based on software renewal
component only. Hardware revenue excluded from NRR calculation.
Confidence: LOW — verify software-only renewal rate with management."

IF NRR not provided AND model is Deep Tech / IP Licensing (Type 10):
Key signal: contract structure (multi-year vs. annual) and whether
licensing fees are fixed or usage-based.
— Multi-year enterprise contracts (3+ years): INFER 95–118%.
  Renewal is near-certain within contract term; expansion comes
  from volume step-ups or additional use cases licensed.
  CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.
— Annual licensing agreements: INFER 85–100%.
  Annual renewal creates churn risk at each cycle.
  CONFIDENCE: LOW.
If contract structure not stated: apply annual benchmark (85–100%)
as the conservative default.
STATE IN OUTPUT: "NRR inferred at [X]% based on [contract type].
Confidence: [level]. Contract length is the primary NRR determinant
for IP licensing — request contract terms to confirm."

IF NRR not provided AND model is Media / Content (Type 9):
Distinguish sub-type before inferring:
— Subscription model: INFER 75–95%.
  Key signal: monthly vs. annual subscription mix.
  Annual subscription = higher NRR confidence (lower near-term churn).
  Monthly churn rate is the primary risk signal; if not disclosed,
  flag as information gap.
  CONFIDENCE: LOW — content quality and algorithm changes make
  retention highly volatile.
— Advertising model: NRR metric is not applicable.
  Use advertiser retention rate as substitute.
  INFER advertiser retention of 60–80% annually.
  CONFIDENCE: LOW.
STATE IN OUTPUT (subscription): "NRR inferred at [X]% based on
[monthly/annual] subscription model. Confidence: LOW. Content
quality and platform changes are material risks to this inference."
STATE IN OUTPUT (advertising): "NRR not applicable for advertising
model. Inferring advertiser retention of [X]%. Confidence: LOW."

─── B3. CAC INFERENCE ───

Step 1 — Identify GTM motion from deal materials:
PLG signals: self-serve sign-up, free tier, product trials, low ACV.
Inbound signals: marketing-led, content/SEO mentioned, SDR team only.
Outbound signals: AE-heavy team, enterprise sales cycle mentioned.
Channel signals: reseller, OEM, or partnership-led distribution.
Enterprise direct signals: RFP process, procurement, >6 month sales cycle.

Step 2 — Apply inference:

IF CAC not provided AND GTM is PLG:
INFER $50–$800 per customer. ACV is the primary signal.
Formula: CAC ≈ ACV × 0.15 to 0.40 for healthy PLG.
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM if team size known; LOW if not.

SALARY BENCHMARKS FOR CAC HEADCOUNT FORMULA (fully loaded, GBP):
SDR:                    £65,000–£85,000
AE SMB:                 £90,000–£120,000
AE Mid-Market:          £120,000–£160,000
Senior AE Enterprise:   £150,000–£200,000
Marketing Head:         £100,000–£140,000
NOTE: For US-based companies, apply 1.3x multiplier to all salary
figures above. For other geographies, adjust accordingly and state
the adjustment in the output.

IF CAC not provided AND GTM is Inbound:
INFER £400–£4,000 per customer.
If headcount data available: CAC = (marketing + SDR salaries × 1.3) ÷
new customers per year. Use £75K fully loaded for SDR; £120K for
marketing head (or apply geography multiplier per note above).
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM if headcount known; LOW if not.

IF CAC not provided AND GTM is Outbound SMB:
INFER £1,500–£10,000.
If AE headcount known: CAC = (AE headcount × £105K fully loaded) ÷
(AE count × 20 deals/year closed rate).
(Apply geography multiplier if US-based: £105K × 1.3 = ~£137K.)
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF CAC not provided AND GTM is Outbound Mid-Market:
INFER £12,000–£50,000.
AE fully loaded cost: £140K–£160K. Expected quota attainment: 70%.
Expected deals per AE per year: 6–15 at mid-market.
(Apply geography multiplier if US-based: £140K–£160K × 1.3.)
CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM.

IF CAC not provided AND GTM is Enterprise Direct:
INFER $50,000–$250,000.
If sales cycle stated: shorter cycles (3–6 months) suggest lower bound;
longer cycles (9–18 months) suggest upper bound or above.
CONFIDENCE: LOW. State: "Enterprise CAC highly variable. Inference
based on deal cycle length and AE cost structure only."

IF CAC not provided AND GTM is Channel/Partnership:
INFER $1,000–$15,000. Apply partner margin (15–35%) reduction to
blended economics.
CONFIDENCE: LOW.

─── B4. LTV INFERENCE ───

APPLY WHEN: ACV known or inferable, gross margin known or inferred,
NRR known or inferred.

Formula: LTV = (ACV × Gross Margin %) ÷ Gross Revenue Churn Rate
Gross Revenue Churn = MAX(0, 1 - NRR) when NRR <100%.
When NRR >100%, use gross logo churn inferred from segment:
— Enterprise: 4–8% annual gross logo churn.
— Mid-Market: 7–14% annual gross logo churn.
— SMB: 15–25% annual gross logo churn.

EXAMPLE INFERENCE CHAIN:
IF ACV = $24,000 AND model is Pure SaaS AND segment is Mid-Market:
— Infer gross margin: 75% (MEDIUM confidence).
— Infer NRR: 108% (MEDIUM confidence).
— Infer gross logo churn: 10% (MEDIUM confidence).
— Calculate LTV: ($24,000 × 0.75) ÷ 0.10 = $180,000.
STATE IN OUTPUT: "LTV inferred at approximately $180K based on inferred
GM of 75%, inferred churn of 10%. Confidence: MEDIUM. Based on two
inference layers — verify with actual gross margin disclosure."

─── B5. PAYBACK PERIOD INFERENCE ───

Formula: Payback (months) = CAC ÷ (ACV ÷ 12 × Gross Margin %)

APPLY INFERRED INPUTS if actuals not available. Always state how many
inference layers are in the calculation.

BENCHMARKS FOR ASSESSMENT:
<12 months: Excellent (flag as positive signal).
12–18 months: Healthy for SMB/Mid-Market.
18–30 months: Acceptable for Enterprise.
>30 months: Concerning for any segment — flag as risk.
>36 months: Red flag. State: "Payback period exceeds 3 years at
inferred unit economics. Business requires sustained external capital
to fund growth — raises structural risk question."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION C — CONFIDENCE CALIBRATION RULES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HIGH CONFIDENCE: Assign when 2 or more independent signals from the
deal materials confirm the inference AND the model type is unambiguous
(does not trigger Section F hybrid rules).
Required language: "[Metric] inferred at [value]. Confidence: HIGH.
Based on [signal 1] and [signal 2]."

MEDIUM CONFIDENCE: Assign when exactly 1 direct company-specific signal
is available OR the model type is partially ambiguous between two types.
Required language: "[Metric] not disclosed. Inferring [value] based on
[single signal] and [model type] category benchmark. Confidence: MEDIUM.
Verify with management."

LOW CONFIDENCE: Assign when inference is based solely on category
benchmark with no company-specific signals, OR when two inference
layers are required (i.e., inferring a metric from another inferred metric).
Required language: "[Metric] inferred from category benchmark only —
no company-specific data available. Confidence: LOW. This inference
should not be the sole basis for the recommendation tier."

RULE: When two or more LOW confidence inferences appear in the same
deal, trigger an Information Gaps flag: "Multiple key unit economics
metrics are inferred at LOW confidence. Recommend requesting [specific
data points] before advancing."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION D — MATERIALITY RULES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

AN INFERENCE MATERIALLY AFFECTS THE RECOMMENDATION WHEN any of these
conditions are true:

Condition 1: The inferred metric, if wrong by ±20%, would change the
G7 score by more than 5 points.
Condition 2: The inferred metric is the primary basis for a RED FLAG
classification (HIGH or MEDIUM severity).
Condition 3: The inferred metric moves the deal between recommendation
tiers (PASS → WATCH, WATCH → PRIORITY REVIEW, or reverse).

WHEN CONDITION IS MET, Alex must append to the recommendation:
"NOTE: This recommendation is conditional on [inferred metric] being
approximately [inferred value]. If actual [metric] is below [lower
threshold], recommendation changes to [lower tier]. Request [specific
data] to confirm before advancing."

AN INFERENCE IS BACKGROUND CONTEXT WHEN:
— The inferred metric is consistent across all plausible scenarios.
— Varying the inference ±30% does not change the recommendation tier.
— The inferred metric confirms an already established pattern and does
   not introduce a new risk or opportunity.
In these cases: state the inference briefly with confidence level and
do not append a conditional note to the recommendation.

SECTION E — GROSS MARGIN VALUATION BRIDGE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RULE: Gross margin — not founder narrative — determines the valuation
framework. Apply this table mechanically on every deal.

GROSS MARGIN → VALUATION FRAMEWORK:
>75%:      Pure SaaS multiples apply. Range: 8–20x ARR.
60–75%:    Discounted SaaS. Range: 6–12x ARR.
45–60%:    Mixed model. Range: 4–8x ARR or 8–12x EBITDA (use lower).
30–45%:    Hardware or services-heavy. Range: 3–5x ARR or 5–8x EBITDA.
<30%:      Physical goods or services. Range: 1–3x Revenue.

WHEN FOUNDER CLAIMS A VALUATION FRAMEWORK INCONSISTENT WITH GROSS MARGIN:
Required language: "Gross margin of [X]% places this business in the
[framework] category. Founder's claimed [stated framework] multiple of
[Y]x is not applicable at this margin structure. Fair value reference
range: [Z1]x–[Z2]x [metric] at [framework] multiples. The [Y]x
valuation implies [gross margin]% margins — [assessment of whether
achievable and on what timeline]."

EXCEPTIONS:
Exception 1: If gross margin is inferred (not actual), append: "Note:
gross margin is inferred. If actual gross margin exceeds 75%, SaaS
multiples apply."
Exception 2: If the company is pre-revenue or <$500K ARR, use the
implied gross margin of the target model state rather than current.
State this explicitly: "Applying target-state gross margin of [X]%
as company is pre-scale. Current blended margin not meaningful."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION F — MODEL TYPE INTERACTION RULES (HYBRID MODELS)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HYBRID IDENTIFICATION RULE:
When a company has revenue characteristics of two model types, identify
PRIMARY model (>60% of revenue, or primary growth driver) and SECONDARY
model. Apply primary model's unit economics framework throughout.
Document secondary model as a modifier — positive or negative.

IF primary vs. secondary split is unclear (45–55% each):
Apply the LOWER gross margin model as primary for valuation purposes
(conservative bias). State: "Revenue split between [Type A] and [Type B]
is unclear. Applying [lower margin type] framework for valuation as
conservative baseline."

COMMON HYBRID PATTERNS AND THEIR MODIFIERS:

SAAS + MARKETPLACE:
Identify: Which generates >60% of revenue — subscriptions or take rate?
If SaaS primary: marketplace component increases NRR. Positive modifier.
Apply 5–10% NRR uplift to SaaS benchmark if marketplace GMV is growing.
If Marketplace primary: SaaS component reduces revenue volatility.
Positive modifier. Discount marketplace take-rate risk by 10–15%.
Unit economics: worse than pure SaaS (lower GM), better than pure
marketplace (predictable base revenue).

USAGE-BASED + SERVICES:
Identify: What % of revenue is committed (contracted) vs. variable?
If committed >60%: treat as Usage-Based primary. Services is a retention tool.
If variable >60%: flag revenue predictability as MEDIUM risk.
Key ratio: committed ARR ÷ total revenue. Below 40% triggers red flag.
Unit economics: worse NRR predictability than Pure SaaS; better LTV
than pure services.

HARDWARE + SAAS:
Identify: Is hardware sold at a margin or subsidised?
If hardware subsidised (sold at or below cost): LTV calculation must
recover hardware subsidy before counting software margin.
Adjusted LTV = Software LTV − Hardware subsidy per unit.
If hardware subsidised and LTV:CAC <3x after subsidy recovery: flag
as structural economics risk.
If hardware at margin: blended model, standard Section B6 applies.
Unit economics: worse than Pure SaaS due to hardware capital intensity
and replacement cycle risk.

MARKETPLACE + TRANSACTION FEE:
Identify: Are the marketplace participants and transaction fee payers
the same entity or different?
If same buyer (e.g. merchant pays listing fee AND transaction fee):
Concentration risk is doubled. Flag if top 10 merchants >40% of GMV.
If different (e.g. marketplace lists products, payment fee charged
to end consumer): Two separate revenue streams. Analyse independently.
Apply blended gross margin. Unit economics: better than single-model
if both sides are growing; worse if one side is subsidising the other.
