ALEX ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK — VERSION 5
FRAMEWORK 5: COHORT DECAY PATTERN LIBRARY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PURPOSE: The shape of a retention curve tells you more about a business
than any single metric. Experienced investors diagnose retention health
from the pattern of available data — even when cohort charts are not
provided. Alex recognises 12 reference retention patterns from partial
data signals and applies the correct diagnostic conclusion to each.

RULE ZERO: Never accept a retention claim at face value. Decompose
it into one of the 12 patterns below using available proxy signals.
If retention data is absent from the submission entirely, state this
explicitly and apply a traction score penalty. Absence of retention
data at revenue stage is itself a signal.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION A — HOW TO READ RETENTION WITHOUT COHORT DATA
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

When cohort charts are absent, Alex uses six proxy signals to infer
retention health. Apply all six. Cross-reference signals — a strong
signal in one proxy and a weak signal in another indicates a nuanced
retention picture, not a clean bill of health.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PROXY SIGNAL 1 — NRR TREND

A single NRR figure is less informative than an NRR trend. When trend
data is available, apply these conclusions:

Rising NRR (increasing quarter-on-quarter): Customers are staying and
  increasing spend. The product creates expansion behaviour. Gross churn
  may exist but expansion revenue from retained customers more than
  compensates. Diagnosis: healthy, and improving.

Flat NRR (stable at a single level for 2+ quarters): Customers are
  staying but not expanding. The product retains but does not grow
  within accounts. The business is dependent on new customer acquisition
  for all revenue growth. Diagnosis: stable but ceiling risk.

Declining NRR (falling quarter-on-quarter): Customers are either
  shrinking their spend, downgrading, or churning. Even if absolute
  revenue is growing (due to new customer addition), declining NRR
  indicates a deteriorating retention base. Diagnosis: retention problem
  masked by top-line growth. Flag explicitly.

Single NRR figure with no trend: State the figure against benchmarks
  in Section C. Note: "NRR trend not provided — single point cannot
  confirm whether retention is improving or declining."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PROXY SIGNAL 2 — REVENUE GROWTH VS CUSTOMER COUNT GROWTH

Compare revenue growth rate to customer count growth rate over the
same period. The relationship between these two rates reveals the
retention dynamic:

Revenue growing faster than customer count: Existing customers are
  expanding spend. NRR is above 100%. Retention is healthy and
  expansion revenue is contributing to growth. Diagnosis: strong.

Revenue and customer count growing at the same rate: No expansion
  revenue. NRR is near 100%. All growth comes from new customer
  acquisition. Retention is adequate but not a growth driver.
  Diagnosis: acceptable — depends on CAC efficiency.

Customer count growing faster than revenue: ACV is declining per
  customer. Either existing customers are downgrading, or the company
  is acquiring lower-value customers to replace churned higher-value
  customers. Diagnosis: warning signal — investigate ACV mix shift.

Revenue growing while customer count is flat or declining: Expansion
  revenue from retained customers is masking gross logo churn. NRR
  may be high while gross churn is also high. Diagnosis: hidden
  retention problem — high NRR can coexist with high logo loss.
  State explicitly: "Revenue growth is masking potential logo churn.
  Gross logo churn rate is required."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PROXY SIGNAL 3 — LOGO CHURN SIGNALS

When explicit churn metrics are absent, these observable signals
from the deck or submission indicate elevated logo churn:

No named reference customers from more than 18 months ago: The
  company cannot produce a customer who has been with them for
  over 18 months and is willing to be named. Response: "Absence
  of reference customers with tenure >18 months is a churn signal.
  Require logo retention data before accepting retention claims."

Customer testimonials are all recent: All quoted customers are
  from the last 6–12 months. No long-tenure customer voices.
  Response: "Testimonial recency suggests either the company is
  new (check founding date) or early customers have churned."

'Growing customer base' language without retention metrics: The
  founder describes an expanding customer base with no reference
  to churn, logo retention, or NRR. Response: "Growth-only language
  without retention data should be treated as undemonstrated
  retention. State: 'Retention not evidenced — growth may be masking
  churn.'"

Reference customers unwilling to be named publicly: Customers exist
  but will not allow public identification. This is either a sector
  norm (financial services, government) or a satisfaction signal.
  Response: "Assess whether non-disclosure is sector-standard or
  an outlier. If the sector routinely uses public references, this
  is a concern."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PROXY SIGNAL 4 — COHORT REVENUE PATTERN

When partial cohort data is provided, apply these inference rules:

If a single cohort's performance is stated (e.g. "customers from
  2022 have increased spend by 40%"): Infer this cohort's NRR is
  approximately 140%. Do not generalise to all cohorts — one cohort's
  performance may be atypical. State: "One cohort evidences strong
  expansion. Full cohort analysis required to confirm this pattern
  is consistent across vintages."

If multiple cohorts are referenced with improving performance over
  time: Pattern 11 (Cohort Improvement) — apply the 1.0x traction
  multiplier and note the positive trajectory.

If multiple cohorts are referenced with flat or declining performance
  over time: Pattern 5 (Slow Bleed) or Pattern 2 (Leaking Bucket) —
  apply the 0.6x multiplier and flag the concern.

If the company references ARR per customer increasing over time:
  Infer expansion NRR above 100%. Treat as corroborating evidence
  for Pattern 3 (Smile Curve) or Pattern 10 (Negative Churn).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PROXY SIGNAL 5 — PRODUCT USAGE SIGNALS

For products where DAU/MAU or equivalent usage frequency data is
provided or inferable, apply these churn risk inferences:

DAU/MAU above 40%: High engagement. Users are returning to the
  product daily relative to monthly actives. Churn risk is low —
  habitual use has formed. Diagnosis: retention is likely strong.

DAU/MAU 20–40%: Moderate engagement. Users are active but not
  daily. For workflow tools, acceptable. For consumer apps, this
  indicates moderate churn risk. Diagnosis: acceptable for B2B,
  requires investigation for consumer.

DAU/MAU below 20%: Weak engagement. Users are active monthly but
  not forming a daily habit. For B2B tools with weekly workflows
  (e.g. reporting tools, board management), this may be expected —
  check against product design intent. For consumer apps or daily
  workflow tools, this is a high churn risk signal.
  Diagnosis: flag and cross-reference with logo churn data.

DAU/MAU not provided for a consumer product at revenue stage:
  Treat as a missing retention signal. State: "DAU/MAU not provided.
  For a consumer product, this is a material omission."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PROXY SIGNAL 6 — CUSTOMER EXPANSION SIGNALS

Observable signals of expansion within existing accounts, and what
each implies for NRR:

Seat count growth per customer mentioned: Implies per-seat pricing
  with expansion motion. NRR likely above 110% if seat growth
  is consistent. Infer NRR: (seat growth rate + 100%).

Additional product lines sold to existing customers: Land-and-expand
  motion is active. NRR above 115% is typical when cross-sell
  is a stated revenue driver. State: "Cross-sell expansion signal
  present — NRR likely above 115%."

Multi-year contracts noted: Customers are committing beyond annual
  terms. Logo churn is mechanically suppressed during contract
  term. NRR is high by definition for contracted periods.
  Flag: "Multi-year contracts suppress churn metrics — gross churn
  at contract renewal is the relevant risk, not in-period churn."

Enterprise customers upgrading tiers: Usage is outgrowing lower
  tiers. Strong product-value signal. NRR above 120% is typical
  in this pattern. Infer Pattern 10 (Negative Churn).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION B — THE 12 RETENTION PATTERNS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 1 — THE IDEAL CURVE

SHAPE: Drops sharply in days 1–30 as new users who never activated
fully drop out. Then flattens and holds near-constant at a stable
level indefinitely. The curve has a clear floor that does not erode.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: Genuine product-market fit. A core user group has
found irreplaceable value in the product and will not leave. The
initial drop is healthy selection — the customers who stayed are the
right customers.

PROXY SIGNALS: NRR flat or rising; logo churn below 5% annually;
DAU/MAU above 40% for consumer; reference customers with 18+ month
tenure named; founder can quote a specific retention floor metric.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: B2B SaaS with deep workflow integration;
consumer social products with strong community; productivity tools
used daily.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern matches the Ideal
Curve — initial dropout followed by stable long-term retention floor.
This is strong evidence of product-market fit in the retained segment.
Retention is a strength in this deal."

RISK LEVEL: LOW.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 2 — THE LEAKING BUCKET

SHAPE: Steady, consistent decline over time with no flattening point.
Every cohort loses customers at a consistent rate — typically 5–10%
monthly — until approaching near-zero.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: No product-market fit. Customers try the product and
leave. The company is growing only because new customer acquisition
exceeds the rate of departure. This is an inherently terminal dynamic.

PROXY SIGNALS: Customer count growing but NRR declining; new customer
acquisition is the only cited growth driver; no long-tenure reference
customers; DAU/MAU falling; founder cannot state a retention floor.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: Consumer apps without a formed habit;
early-stage B2B products that have not found the right ICP;
products solving a one-time need rather than a recurring need.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern matches the Leaking
Bucket — consistent ongoing churn with no stabilisation. This is the
defining signal of absent product-market fit. Revenue growth from new
acquisition is masking a terminal retention dynamic. Traction score
is reduced to reflect this risk."

RISK LEVEL: CRITICAL.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 3 — THE SMILE CURVE

SHAPE: Initial drop from day one, stabilisation, then gradual upward
movement in cohort revenue over time as retained customers expand
their usage and spend.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: Customers who stay become more deeply engaged and
more valuable over time. Expansion revenue builds gradually within
cohorts. The longer the customer tenure, the more they spend.

PROXY SIGNALS: NRR trending upward over time; revenue growing faster
than customer count; seat expansion or tier upgrades referenced;
ACV per customer increasing as tenure increases.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: B2B SaaS with usage-based or seat-based
pricing; platforms with a land-and-expand model; tools that replace
manual processes incrementally.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern matches the Smile
Curve — initial stabilisation followed by expansion within retained
cohorts. This is a strong commercial signal: long-tenure customers
are the company's best growth lever. NRR above 120% is consistent
with this pattern."

RISK LEVEL: LOW.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 4 — THE CLIFF

SHAPE: Near-100% retention for a fixed period — typically the contract
term of 12 or 24 months — followed by a sudden, sharp drop at renewal.
Retention within the contract term is excellent. Renewal rate is poor.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: Customers are not leaving during the contract because
exit costs are high — contractual, operational, or financial. But when
given a structured renewal decision point, they do not commit again.
The product does not justify renewal on its merits.

PROXY SIGNALS: Logo retention appears strong in-year; renewal rates
are not provided or are below 70%; founder emphasises annual contract
value but not multi-year renewals; NRR declines sharply in Q4 or at
calendar year-end; no multi-year contracts mentioned.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: B2B SaaS with annual contracts and high
switching costs during the term; enterprise software with long
implementation cycles; compliance or regulatory tools that customers
adopt mandatorily.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern shows Cliff risk —
strong in-contract retention but renewal behaviour is not evidenced.
In-period churn metrics are not sufficient; renewal rate is the
relevant metric. State: 'Renewal rate required before retention
assessment can be confirmed.'"

RISK LEVEL: HIGH.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 5 — THE SLOW BLEED

SHAPE: Gradual, consistent decline that is too slow to be alarming
in any single quarter — typically 1–3% monthly — but compounds into
significant cohort loss over 18–24 months. The curve has no floor.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: Customers use the product but do not love it.
Switching costs or inertia are keeping them — not product value.
When a sufficiently compelling alternative appears, they will leave.

PROXY SIGNALS: Monthly churn stated between 1–3% but no NRR provided;
customer tenure data absent; founder describes "some churn but it is
improving" without data; long-tenure reference customers rare despite
the company being 2+ years old.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: B2B SaaS with moderate switching costs but
weak product differentiation; productivity tools in competitive
categories; services wrapped in software.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern suggests Slow Bleed —
gradual ongoing churn without a retention floor. At [stated churn
rate]% monthly, approximately [calculate: 1-(1-rate)^12 × 100]% of
any cohort is lost within 12 months. This is below benchmark for
[business model type]. Traction score is reduced."

RISK LEVEL: HIGH.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 6 — THE HOCKEY STICK RETENTION

SHAPE: Early cohorts show poor retention — consistent with Leaking
Bucket or Slow Bleed. More recent cohorts show materially better
retention. The improvement is visible and consistent across successive
cohort vintages.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: The company has found product-market fit after
an early period of iteration. The current product retains well.
Early cohort data should not be used to judge the current state.

PROXY SIGNALS: Founder explicitly references a pivot or product
overhaul at a specific point in time; early NRR was poor but recent
NRR is strong; recent cohort retention data provided and contrasted
with historical performance; customer testimonials are skewed toward
the last 12 months.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: Post-pivot SaaS companies; products that
found their ICP after initial broad launch; companies that narrowed
from a broad consumer product to a focused B2B product.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern shows Hockey Stick
Retention improvement — early cohorts underperformed, recent cohorts
are materially better. This signals product-market fit being found.
Apply the current cohort's retention metrics, not historical averages.
Note: the pivot point and what specifically changed must be evidenced."

RISK LEVEL: MEDIUM (watch for reversion).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 7 — THE POWER USER CORE

SHAPE: Large initial drop — 70–80% of users leave within the first
30 days. Then a very stable, highly engaged remainder who almost
never leave.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: The product has found a small, intensely loyal user
base. The large early dropout is users who tried but were not the
right fit. The retained core is extremely valuable individually.
Business quality depends on whether the retained core is large
enough to build a business on.

PROXY SIGNALS: Low total customer count despite strong NRR from
retained customers; founder cites high engagement metrics from
a small user base; DAU/MAU above 60% for the active cohort;
word-of-mouth growth with very low conversion from trial.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: Professional tools for a specific workflow;
niche B2B products with a narrow ICP; creator economy tools;
developer tools.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern is Power User Core —
high dropout from the total addressable trial population, but
exceptional retention and engagement in the retained segment. The
quality of retention is high. The strategic question is whether the
addressable market of customers who fit the retained profile is
sufficient for the intended scale."

RISK LEVEL: MEDIUM (market size risk, not retention risk).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 8 — THE SEASONAL PATTERN

SHAPE: Regular periodic drops and recoveries corresponding to
business cycles, seasonal demand, or annual contract renewals.
The pattern is predictable and repeating — not trending down.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: The business has structural seasonality. Revenue
and customer counts oscillate predictably. This is not a retention
problem — it is a business model characteristic that must be
accounted for in growth projections and cash flow planning.

PROXY SIGNALS: Revenue stated as showing seasonal patterns; customer
count references a specific period of high acquisition (e.g.
Q4, academic year start); monthly revenue chart shows regular peaks
and troughs at consistent intervals.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: EdTech; hospitality and travel software;
tax and accounting tools; retail-adjacent B2B software; event
management platforms.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern shows Seasonal
behaviour — regular periodic variation consistent with [industry
cycle]. This is not a retention concern but requires seasonality
adjustment in all revenue projections. Year-on-year comparison is
the appropriate metric, not sequential quarter-on-quarter."

RISK LEVEL: LOW (if pattern is stable and understood).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 9 — THE ENGAGEMENT COLLAPSE

SHAPE: Strong retention in the first 30–45 days as users engage with
the new product, followed by a sudden sharp drop at 60–90 days as
novelty fades and habitual use never forms.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: Initial engagement is driven by onboarding,
new-user curiosity, or implementation momentum — not genuine product
value. The habit formation window closes without the habit forming.

PROXY SIGNALS: Strong D7 or D14 retention cited alongside weak D90
retention; "activation" metrics cited without "retention" metrics;
high trial-to-paid conversion but significant churn at 90 days;
DAU/MAU declining steadily after an initial strong period.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: Consumer apps with strong onboarding but
weak core loops; B2B tools that automate a one-time setup process
but have no recurring use case; wellness, productivity, or learning
apps.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern suggests Engagement
Collapse — strong early engagement followed by sharp dropout before
the 90-day mark. This indicates the habit loop has not formed.
Short-term retention metrics overstate the product's long-term
retention health. D90 and D180 retention are the relevant benchmarks."

RISK LEVEL: HIGH.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 10 — THE NEGATIVE CHURN PATTERN

SHAPE: Revenue from a cohort increases over time despite some logo
churn, because expansion revenue from retained customers materially
exceeds lost revenue from churned customers. The cohort's revenue
line moves up and to the right.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: The product is genuinely excellent in B2B terms.
Customers find increasing value and expand their usage. This is the
highest-quality retention signal in B2B. NRR is consistently above
120% in this pattern.

PROXY SIGNALS: NRR stated above 120%; revenue growing materially
faster than customer count; expansion revenue cited explicitly as
a revenue driver; seat or usage growth per customer referenced;
ACV per customer increasing each year; multi-year contracts with
upsell clauses.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: Enterprise SaaS with usage-based or seat-based
pricing; platforms with strong land-and-expand; infrastructure software
with consumption pricing.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern is Negative Churn —
cohort revenue increases over time even accounting for logo attrition.
NRR of [stated figure]% confirms expansion within retained accounts
more than offsets any lost customers. This is the strongest retention
signal in B2B. It is a significant positive factor in the traction
assessment."

RISK LEVEL: LOW.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 11 — THE COHORT IMPROVEMENT PATTERN

SHAPE: Each successive customer cohort shows better retention than
the cohort before it. The company's most recently acquired customers
retain better than customers acquired 12 months ago.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: The product is improving consistently. Onboarding
is getting better. ICP targeting is improving. This is a strong signal
of operational excellence — the team is systematically addressing
the causes of churn.

PROXY SIGNALS: Founder explicitly references improving retention
metrics over time with data; recent cohort NRR materially above
older cohort NRR; D30 or D90 retention improving across consecutive
quarters; churn rate declining consistently.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: Any B2B SaaS post-Series A with a mature
product team; consumer apps with strong data science capability;
marketplaces actively improving supply and demand matching.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern shows consistent
Cohort Improvement — each new cohort retaining better than the last.
This signals systematic product and onboarding improvement. The
business is learning faster than it is churning. This is a strong
operational signal alongside the retention data."

RISK LEVEL: LOW.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PATTERN 12 — THE FALSE FLOOR

SHAPE: Retention appears to flatten at a healthy level — for example,
70% — for several months, creating the impression of a stable
retention floor. Then drops further at 12–18 months as customers who
had been planning to leave finally complete migration.

WHAT IT SIGNALS: Switching costs are creating temporary retention.
Customers are not retained by product value — they are retained by
migration friction. When the friction is overcome, the floor collapses.
The apparent floor is an artefact of switching cost, not product love.

PROXY SIGNALS: Retention claimed at a specific level but NRR declining
simultaneously — customers are staying but reducing spend as they
wind down usage; customer success team noted as "helping with renewals"
rather than driving expansion; reference customers cite switching costs
when asked why they stay, not product value; churn spikes at contract
end in multi-year deals.

COMMON BUSINESS TYPES: B2B SaaS with high data or workflow lock-in
in competitive categories; legacy infrastructure with modern wrappers;
products in categories where a major new entrant has recently appeared.

ALEX'S DIAGNOSTIC CONCLUSION: "Retention pattern suggests False Floor
risk — apparent retention stability may be switching-cost driven rather
than value-driven. The concurrent pattern of [flat retention + declining
NRR / reference customers citing inertia / competitor entry] indicates
the floor may not hold at renewal. Gross churn at renewal and customer
satisfaction metrics are required to confirm."

RISK LEVEL: HIGH.

SECTION C — RETENTION BENCHMARKS BY BUSINESS MODEL
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Apply these benchmarks when assessing whether observed or inferred
retention is strong, acceptable, or a concern. State the benchmark
explicitly in the traction section alongside the observed metric.

B2B SAAS — ENTERPRISE (ACV >£50K):
Annual logo retention: >90% = strong / 85–90% = acceptable / <85% = concern.
NRR: >120% = strong / 110–120% = acceptable / <110% = concern.
Gross revenue churn: <8% annually = acceptable / >8% = concern.

B2B SAAS — MID-MARKET (ACV £10–50K):
Annual logo retention: >85% = strong / 80–85% = acceptable / <80% = concern.
NRR: >115% = strong / 105–115% = acceptable / <105% = concern.
Gross revenue churn: <12% annually = acceptable / >12% = concern.

B2B SAAS — SMB (ACV <£10K):
Monthly logo churn: <2% = strong / 2–3% = acceptable / >3% = concern.
Annual equivalent: <22% strong / 22–31% acceptable / >31% concern.
NRR: >110% = strong / 100–110% = acceptable / <100% = concern.

CONSUMER / MOBILE APP:
D30 retention: >25% = strong / 15–25% = acceptable / <15% = concern.
D90 retention: >15% = strong / 10–15% = acceptable / <10% = concern.
D180 retention: >10% = strong / 6–10% = acceptable / <6% = concern.
DAU/MAU: >40% = strong / 20–40% = acceptable / <20% = concern.

MARKETPLACE — SUPPLY SIDE:
Annual active supplier retention: >80% = strong / 70–80% = acceptable
  / <70% = concern.
Annual GMV retention from existing suppliers: >90% = strong / 80–90%
  = acceptable / <80% = concern.

MARKETPLACE — DEMAND SIDE:
Annual repeat buyer rate: >60% = strong / 45–60% = acceptable
  / <45% = concern.
30-day repeat purchase rate (high-frequency): >35% = strong / 20–35%
  = acceptable / <20% = concern.

E-COMMERCE / DTC:
12-month cohort revenue retention: >50% = strong / 35–50% = acceptable
  / <35% = concern.
Repeat purchase rate within 12 months: >40% = strong / 25–40%
  = acceptable / <25% = concern.
LTV:CAC: >3x = strong / 2–3x = acceptable / <2x = concern.

SECTION D — TRACTION SCORE IMPACT RULES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Traction is scored out of 20. Identify the retention pattern first,
then apply the multiplier to the raw traction score derived from
revenue, growth rate, and customer count.

PATTERNS 1 (Ideal Curve), 3 (Smile Curve), 10 (Negative Churn),
11 (Cohort Improvement):
Multiplier: 1.0x. Full score for traction level achieved.
Add note: "Retention pattern is [pattern name] — healthy. No traction
score reduction applied."

PATTERNS 6 (Hockey Stick Retention), 7 (Power User Core),
8 (Seasonal Pattern):
Multiplier: 0.9x. Minor reduction for pattern-specific caveat.
Add note: "Retention pattern is [pattern name]. Healthy with caveat:
[Pattern 6: confirm improvement is sustained / Pattern 7: market
size risk / Pattern 8: seasonality-adjust projections]. Traction
score reduced by 10% to reflect this uncertainty."

PATTERNS 2 (Leaking Bucket), 4 (Cliff), 5 (Slow Bleed),
9 (Engagement Collapse), 12 (False Floor):
Multiplier: 0.6x. Significant reduction for active retention concern.
Add note: "Retention concern identified — [pattern name] pattern.
Traction score reduced to 60% of raw score. Revenue growth does not
offset retention risk at this stage. Recommend: [Pattern 2: PMF
validation before further investment / Pattern 4: renewal rate
data required / Pattern 5: ICP tightening and churn root cause
analysis / Pattern 9: D90+ retention improvement evidence /
Pattern 12: gross churn at renewal data required]."

RETENTION DATA ABSENT (for a company at revenue stage):
Multiplier: 0.7x. Penalise omission.
Add note: "Retention data not provided for a company with [£X] ARR.
This is a material omission. Traction score reduced by 30% pending
retention evidence. No positive retention assumption applied."

SECTION E — PMF EVIDENCE RULES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Product-Market Fit is not a label. It is a specific set of retention
behaviours. Apply these rules to determine whether PMF is evidenced,
claimed, or absent.

PMF EVIDENCED (requires at least 2 of the following):
— Retention pattern matches Pattern 1 (Ideal Curve), Pattern 3
  (Smile Curve), or Pattern 10 (Negative Churn), confirmed from data.
— NRR above 120% with trend data showing it is stable or improving.
— Organic or word-of-mouth referral accounts for more than 25%
  of new customer acquisition — confirmed, not estimated.
— DAU/MAU above 40% for consumer products, confirmed with data.
— D30 retention above 35% for consumer products.
— Annual logo churn below 5% for B2B products, confirmed for at
  least two consecutive years.
Required output: "PMF evidenced: [list the 2+ criteria met with
specific figures]. Retention is a competitive strength."

PMF CLAIMED BUT NOT EVIDENCED:
— Founder states PMF directly without providing retention metrics.
— A single metric is cited (e.g. NRR only) without corroborating
  retention signals meeting the PMF threshold.
— Usage is described qualitatively ("customers love it," "strong
  engagement") without data meeting the criteria above.
— Sean Ellis survey result cited without retention data.
Required output: "PMF claimed but not evidenced by available data.
[Criteria cited] is consistent with PMF but insufficient alone.
[State what specific additional data is required to confirm.]"

PMF ABSENT:
— Pattern 2 (Leaking Bucket) or Pattern 9 (Engagement Collapse)
  is identified.
— Annual logo churn above 15% for B2B.
— D30 retention below 10% for consumer.
— NRR below 90% confirmed.
— Customer count declining despite active marketing spend.
Required output: "PMF not evidenced by available data. [Specific
metric or pattern] indicates customers are not retaining at the
level required for sustainable growth. This is the primary risk
factor in the traction assessment. Investment thesis requires
a specific PMF development plan and milestone before next capital
deployment is warranted."
