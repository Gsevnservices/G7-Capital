SECTOR 1 — B2B SaaS
Full library: alex_sector_v1_b2b_saas.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Subscription revenue. Business entities as primary paying customers.
Software delivered via browser, API, or mobile. ACV stated or inferable.
Sub-categories: Horizontal SaaS (cross-industry, per-seat); Vertical SaaS
(industry-specific, high switching cost, small TAM ceiling risk);
Developer Tools / Infrastructure (PLG motion, usage-based pricing);
AI-Native SaaS (inference cost is direct COGS — gross margin requires
decomposition); Workflow Automation (replaces measurable manual process,
ROI must be quantifiable).

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
NRR:           STRONG >125% (Series A) | FAIL <95%
Gross margin:  STRONG >80% (Series A) | FAIL <60%
Burn multiple: STRONG <1.5x | FAIL >4x
Logo churn:    STRONG <5% annual (Series A) | FAIL >15%
Magic Number:  STRONG >0.75 | FAIL <0.25
Note: Always decompose blended gross margin — services revenue mixed with
software revenue is the most common early-stage metric manipulation.

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
HIGH — Booked Not Recognised ARR: ARR includes multi-year TCV booked upfront;
request month-by-month MRR schedule.
HIGH — NRR Including New Logos: NRR inflated by adding new customers to the
cohort mid-period; true NRR may be 20–30 points lower.
HIGH — Enterprise Pipeline That Is All Pilots: 5+ enterprise logos listed as
"in pipeline" are all unpaid POCs — zero commercial commitment.
HIGH — Burn Multiple Above 4x With Declining Growth: two consecutive quarters
of rising burn and falling growth is the CAC Spiral in early formation.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The Churn Cliff: 25–40% logo churn hits at month 12–18 when the first mass
renewal cycle arrives — invisible until it lands.
The PLG-to-Enterprise Transition Failure: enterprise sales team hired before
enterprise product features (SSO, RBAC, audit logs) are shipped — pipeline
burns, nothing closes.
The CAC Spiral: early organic/network channels saturate; paid CAC rises 30–50%
year-on-year while LTV is unchanged; burn multiple exceeds 4x.

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"Is your NRR calculated excluding customers added during the measurement period?"
Weak answer: cannot confirm the cohort definition.
"What was your average sales cycle 12 months ago vs today?"
Weak answer: cannot state a number or says "it varies."
"Of your last 20 closings, how many required you personally in the sales process?"
Weak answer: "most" or "all" — CAC is structurally understated.
"What percentage of customers renewed at their first annual renewal?"
Weak answer: no first-renewal data exists yet (churn cliff risk).

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
UK GDPR: data processing agreements required for every B2B SaaS product
handling personal data; non-standard DPA triggers legal delays.
EU AI Act (from August 2026): AI-native SaaS in high-risk categories
(employment, credit, critical infrastructure) requires conformity assessment
before EU deployment.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTOR 2 — FINTECH
Full library: alex_sector_v2_fintech.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Revenue model touches financial transactions, lending, insurance, investment,
or compliance. Regulatory licence is a business prerequisite, not a future plan.
Sub-categories: Payments Infrastructure (take rate, TPV); Neobank / Challenger
Bank (NIM, CAC, deposit growth); Lending / Credit (loss rate, NIM, cost of
capital); Insurtech (combined ratio, GWP, loss ratio); Wealthtech (AUM, take
rate, revenue per account); Regtech / Compliance (ARR, renewal rate);
Open Banking / API Infrastructure (API call volume, revenue per call);
Embedded Finance / BaaS (revenue share, partner count, TPV per partner).

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
Payments — Take Rate: STRONG >0.5% of TPV | FAIL <0.15% (commoditised)
Lending — Loan Loss Rate: STRONG <2% of loan book | FAIL >6% (credit model broken)
Lending — Net Interest Margin: STRONG >4% | FAIL <1.5% (unviable economics)
Insurtech — Combined Ratio: STRONG <90% | FAIL >110% (loss-making underwriting)
Neobank — CAC: STRONG <£30 | FAIL >£150 (unprofitable acquisition)
For Regtech/SaaS-model fintechs: apply B2B SaaS benchmarks alongside these.

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
CRITICAL — Regulatory Licence Risk: product requires FCA authorisation and the
company does not hold the correct licence or is relying on a partner's permissions
— this is the most common fintech deal killer.
HIGH — Lending Loss Rate Understatement: loss rate reported on current book
(short vintage) — true loss rate only visible at 12–18 month loan maturity.
HIGH — Take Rate Compression Below 0.2%: payments business trading on volume
with near-zero margin has no path to profitability without scale that requires
hundreds of millions in capital.
HIGH — Regulatory Capital Deficiency: bank, e-money, or payment institution
without sufficient regulatory capital buffer is one enforcement action from
a wind-down requirement.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The Regulatory Licence Trap: product launches under an agent or partnership
arrangement; principal firm withdraws authorisation; product must cease overnight.
The Credit Cycle Cliff: loan book performs well in benign conditions; first
recession or rate rise exposes under-reserved losses that eliminate the equity buffer.
The BaaS Dependency: entire revenue model runs through one banking partner's
infrastructure; partner exits the relationship or fails — product has no fallback.

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"What FCA permissions do you hold and are you the principal or an appointed
representative?" Weak answer: confusion about permission scope or relies on a partner.
"What is your loan loss rate at 18-month loan maturity, not current book?"
Weak answer: only has current book data; 18-month vintage not yet seasoned.
"What is your take rate and how has it trended over 12 months?"
Weak answer: declining take rate with no explanation or competitive pressure acknowledged.
"What is your cost of capital and how does it change if wholesale funding rates rise 200bps?"
Weak answer: has not stress-tested the funding model against a rate shock.

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
FCA Authorisation: EMI licence (6–12 months), Payment Institution (4–9 months),
Banking Licence (18–36 months, £5M+ minimum capital) — unlicensed products
cannot process payments or hold customer funds in the UK.
Consumer Duty (FCA, July 2023): all retail-facing fintechs must demonstrate
good customer outcomes; non-compliance is an FCA enforcement trigger.
BNPL Regulation (incoming UK): Buy Now Pay Later products will require
Consumer Credit Act authorisation — unregulated BNPL business models face
mandatory restructuring.

SECTOR 3 — HEALTHTECH
Full library: alex_sector_v3_healthtech.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Product addresses clinical decision-making, patient data, medical devices,
or NHS procurement. Regulatory approval (MHRA, FDA) is required before sale.
Sub-categories: Clinical Decision Support (CDSS); EHR / Clinical Workflow;
Digital Therapeutics (DTx — requires clinical evidence); Health Data / Analytics;
Medtech / Medical Devices (MHRA regulated); Healthtech Enablers (infrastructure
for health providers); D2C Health (consumer-facing, no clinical claims);
Health Insurance Tech (claims, underwriting). NHS sales cycle benchmarks:
NHS Trust 12–18 months; ICB 18–24 months; Private Hospital 6–15 months;
GP Practice 3–9 months.

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
Clinical evidence level: STRONG: RCT published peer-reviewed | FAIL: no clinical
validation, safety claims made without evidence
MHRA device class: Class I (self-cert) | Class IIa/IIb (UKCA conformity) |
Class III (Notified Body approval required before any UK sale)
NHS pipeline conversion: STRONG >30% of formal evaluations to contract |
FAIL <10% (procurement navigation failure)
NRR (NHS contracts): STRONG >115% | FAIL <100% (NHS contracts that do not
expand are a renewal risk — NHS frameworks are re-tendered)
D2C retention (30-day): STRONG >40% | FAIL <20% for health/wellness apps

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
CRITICAL — Regulatory Approval Gap: medical device or DTx product is in clinical
use without MHRA registration or UKCA marking — immediate enforcement risk.
HIGH — NHS Pilot That Never Converts: 5+ NHS pilots described as "in evaluation"
with no signed commercial agreements — pilots are not pipeline.
HIGH — Clinical Claims Without Evidence: product makes clinical outcome claims
(reduces readmissions, improves diagnosis accuracy) that are not supported by
a peer-reviewed study or NICE-recognised clinical trial.
HIGH — Data Governance Failure: product handles NHS patient data without DSPT
completion and National Data Opt-Out compliance — procurement is blocked.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The NHS Pilot Graveyard: product accumulates 10–20 NHS pilots over 3 years and
converts none to commercial contracts — the NHS evaluation process has been
mistaken for a sales funnel.
The Regulatory Reclassification: product launched as a wellness app (unregulated);
MHRA reclassifies it as a medical device mid-market; retrofit compliance takes
18 months and blocks all new sales.
The NICE Evidence Gap: DTx product gains traction with early-adopter NHS trusts;
scale-up requires NICE approval; NICE evidence standard requires a full RCT;
the company has neither the time nor the capital to fund it.

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"What is your MHRA device classification and do you have UKCA marking in place?"
Weak answer: uncertain of classification; UKCA not started.
"How many NHS pilots have converted to paid contracts in the last 24 months?"
Weak answer: many pilots, zero or one conversion — the conversion engine is broken.
"Is your DSPT complete and have you mapped compliance with National Data Opt-Out?"
Weak answer: DSPT in progress; opt-out compliance not addressed.
"What clinical evidence underpins your outcome claims — is it peer-reviewed?"
Weak answer: internal data only; no peer-reviewed publication; "we're working on it."

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
MHRA / UKCA: Class IIa and above medical devices require Notified Body conformity
assessment before UK market access — no approval, no NHS sale.
DTAC (Digital Technology Assessment Criteria): NHS England DTAC assessment
is required before NHS procurement of digital health tools — takes 3–6 months.
NICE Evidence Standards Framework (ESF): Tier D (highest) evidence requirement
for DTx seeking NHS commissioning — requires peer-reviewed RCT, frequently
the single largest capital requirement for a healthtech company.

SECTOR 4 — MARKETPLACE
Full library: alex_sector_v4_marketplace.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Revenue is a take rate on transactions between two or more parties. The company
does not own the inventory or the labour it sells. Network effects are the primary
moat claim.
Core equation: GMV × Take Rate = Gross Revenue → Contribution Margin.
Sub-categories: B2C Marketplace (goods, 12–20% take rate); B2B Marketplace
(8–18% take rate, longer sales cycle); Labour / Services Marketplace (15–30%
take rate, worker classification risk); Financial Marketplace (regulated);
Real Estate / Property (NTSELAT material information rules); Healthcare Marketplace
(CQC registration for care services); Horizontal Platform (multi-category,
lowest take rate, network density is key).
Liquidity Threshold: the minimum supply/demand density for self-sustainability.
Below this threshold, the marketplace is a subsidy business, not a network.

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
GMV growth YoY: STRONG >100% (seed) / >60% (Series A) | FAIL <25%
Take rate:      STRONG stable or expanding | FAIL declining >1pp year-on-year
Contribution margin (post-fulfilment): STRONG >20% of GMV | FAIL <0% (subsidised)
Liquidity rate (transactions per active listing): STRONG >50% monthly |
FAIL <20% (illiquid marketplace — demand cannot find supply reliably)
Repeat purchase rate: STRONG >60% of buyers transact again within 12 months |
FAIL <30% (one-sided value — no retention loop)

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
HIGH — Disintermediation Rate Above 5%: buyers and sellers are completing
transactions off-platform after first contact — the take rate has no moat.
HIGH — GMV Inflated by Subsidised Transactions: marketplace uses incentives,
cashback, or promotional pricing to inflate GMV; true market-rate GMV is
materially lower.
HIGH — Single-Side Dominance Without Cross-Side: supply (or demand) is concentrated
in one city, one category, or one customer — the network is not a network, it is
a single relationship.
MEDIUM — Take Rate Compression Trend: take rate declining quarter-on-quarter
signals competitive pressure or seller power accumulation — the margin is being
competed away.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The Liquidity Trap: marketplace launches with broad geography or category coverage
at low density in each; neither buyers nor sellers find reliable matches; the company
subsidises transactions to create activity rather than building genuine liquidity.
The Disintermediation Collapse: once buyers and sellers have made contact on the
platform, they transact directly — the marketplace loses the repeat transaction
revenue it modelled for; this is most common in high-ACV, low-frequency categories.
The Regulatory Reclassification (Labour): marketplace classifies workers as
self-employed; regulatory or court ruling reclassifies them as employees; cost
base increases 30–50%; unit economics collapse (UK Uber ruling precedent).

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"What percentage of transactions that start on your platform are completed on
your platform?" Weak answer: does not track disintermediation; assumes it is low.
"What is your take rate and has it changed in the last 12 months?"
Weak answer: declining with no clear explanation; or take rate is not charged yet.
"What is your liquidity rate — what percentage of active listings transact in
a given month?" Weak answer: does not measure liquidity at listing level.
"What is your buyer repeat rate within 12 months?"
Weak answer: cannot state; or below 30% with no plan to improve.

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
UK Uber Ruling (Supreme Court 2021): gig economy labour marketplaces face
worker reclassification as employees — review worker status before investing.
DAC7 / HMRC Seller Reporting: digital marketplaces must report seller income
to HMRC from January 2024 — operational compliance requirement with penalties.
NTSELAT Material Information (property): property marketplaces must display
material information (tenure, council tax, utilities) at point of listing —
non-compliant listings risk Trading Standards enforcement.

SECTOR 5 — DEEP TECH
Full library: alex_sector_v5_deeptech.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Product requires 3+ years of fundamental R&D before commercial deployment.
Standard SaaS metrics do not apply pre-revenue. Use TRL (Technology Readiness
Level) framework: TRL 1–3 (basic research), TRL 4–6 (validation), TRL 7–9
(commercial deployment). Investable at seed: TRL 4+ with credible path to TRL 7.
MODIFIED FRAMEWORK: revenue traction → validation signals; NRR → IP portfolio;
CAC/LTV → capital-to-milestone ratio; burn multiple → capital to first commercial revenue.
Sub-categories: Advanced Materials; Semiconductors / Chips (fabless £15–60M capital);
Robotics / Automation; Quantum Computing / Sensing (£20–100M+ capital);
Space Technology (£30–200M+ capital, CAA licensing); Cleantech Hardware;
Biotech / Synthetic Biology (UK Precision Breeding Act divergence from EU GMO);
Deep Tech Software (AI foundation models, simulation).

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
TRL at pitch:   STRONG TRL 6–7 (prototype validated in relevant environment) |
                FAIL TRL 1–3 (still basic research — too early for most VC)
Non-revenue validation tier:
  Tier 1 (investable signals): paid pilot, defence contract, LOI with stated value,
  strategic equity investment from industry partner.
  Tier 2 (supporting signals): Innovate UK / ARIA grant, peer-reviewed publication,
  SBRI contract.
  Tier 3 (insufficient alone): patent filing, academic collaboration, competition win.
Capital to first commercial revenue: STRONG <£10M | CONCERN £10–30M | FAIL >£50M
  (unless Quantum or Space — adjust ceiling accordingly)
IP portfolio: STRONG: granted patents in key markets + freedom-to-operate analysis |
FAIL: trade secret only with no patent protection and large incumbents in the space.
Wright's Law (hardware): BoM cost should decline 15–30% per doubling of cumulative
production — if cost curve is flat, manufacturing scale-up is not working.

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
HIGH — TRL Overstated: company claims TRL 7–8 but has only demonstrated in
lab conditions (TRL 5) — the gap between lab and deployment is where most deep
tech companies die.
HIGH — Capital to Commercial Revenue Exceeds Available Runway: the path from
current TRL to first paying customer requires more capital than the current raise
provides — the company will need another round before generating revenue.
HIGH — Single University IP Dependency: all core IP is licensed from one
university; licence terms, exclusivity scope, and field-of-use restrictions
have not been fully reviewed — this is a structural risk to the business.
HIGH — NSI Act Mandatory Notification Not Filed: foreign acquisition of UK
company in any of the 17 NSI-sensitive sectors without BEIS notification is
void by law — a deal-level legal risk if acquirer is non-UK.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The Valley of Death: company achieves TRL 6 on grant funding; cannot raise
Series A without revenue; cannot generate revenue without Series A capital —
stranded between research and commercialisation.
The Manufacturing Scale-Up Cost Spiral: unit economics at pilot scale (100 units)
do not translate to production scale; BoM cost does not fall as predicted by
Wright's Law; gross margin at scale is 20 points below model (Northvolt pattern).
The Export Control Trap: UK company uses US-origin technology or components;
ITAR extraterritorial reach applies; company cannot sell to certain international
customers or share IP with non-US-approved parties without US State Department
licence — discovered only when a foreign customer is identified.

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"What is your current TRL and what has been demonstrated in a real-world
environment — not a lab?" Weak answer: conflates lab results with deployment-ready
performance; no real-world validation exists.
"What is your capital requirement to reach first commercial revenue?"
Weak answer: states a number without a milestone-by-milestone breakdown;
or the number is below what the TRL gap realistically requires.
"Do you have freedom-to-operate analysis covering your key markets?"
Weak answer: has not commissioned FTO; relies on the fact that patents are filed.
"Does your technology involve any US-origin components, software, or research
collaboration?" Weak answer: unaware of ITAR implications; no export control
review conducted.

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
NSI Act 2021 (UK National Security and Investment): mandatory government
notification required for foreign acquisitions in 17 sectors including AI,
advanced materials, quantum, semiconductors, space, synthetic biology, defence —
non-notified transactions are void.
ITAR (US International Traffic in Arms Regulations): extraterritorial reach
applies to UK companies using US-origin defence or dual-use technology — restricts
international sales and information sharing without US export licence.
UK Space Industry Act 2018: CAA launch licence required for any UK space launch
operation; in-orbit operations require additional regulatory framework compliance.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTOR 6 — CONSUMER
Full library: alex_sector_v6_consumer.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Primary payer is an individual consumer, not a business entity. Revenue model
is subscription, in-app purchase, advertising, or direct-to-consumer product sale.
THE MOST IMPORTANT CONSUMER METRIC: organic share of new user acquisition
in last 90 days. Below 30% organic = paid media business, not a product moat.
Sub-categories: Consumer Mobile App; Consumer Subscription (Digital);
DTC Physical (return rate and post-returns gross margin are critical);
Consumer Marketplace (apply Marketplace Library alongside this one);
Consumer Fintech (apply Fintech Library alongside this one);
Consumer Health / Wellness (apply Healthtech Library for clinical claims);
Gaming / Entertainment.

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
D30 retention (by product type):
  Social: STRONG >40% | FAIL <20%
  Utility: STRONG >35% | FAIL <15%
  Health/wellness: STRONG >30% | FAIL <12%
  Gaming: STRONG >25% | FAIL <10%
DAU/MAU ratio: Social STRONG >60% | Entertainment STRONG >30% |
Utility STRONG >25% | Health STRONG >20% | Finance STRONG >8%
K-factor (viral coefficient): STRONG >0.5 | Genuinely viral >1.0 | FAIL <0.1
Free-to-paid conversion: STRONG >8% | ACCEPTABLE 3–8% | FAIL <2%
Physical DTC — post-returns gross margin: STRONG >50% | FAIL <35%
CAC by channel: Meta <£8 | TikTok <£5 | Google UAC <£10 | Organic <£2

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
HIGH — Paid Media Dependency: organic share of new user acquisition below 30%
— the product has no word-of-mouth engine; growth stops the moment paid spend stops.
HIGH — D30 Retention Below Category Benchmark: early retention is the single
best predictor of LTV and word-of-mouth; below-benchmark D30 is almost never
fixed by product iteration alone.
HIGH — Physical DTC Return Rate Above Category Benchmark: fashion >35%, beauty
>12% — above-benchmark returns collapse gross margin and signal product-market
fit failure.
MEDIUM — CAC Payback Exceeding 18 Months on Monthly Subscription: consumer
churn rates make 18+ month payback periods structurally unprofitable — the
cohort will have churned before CAC is recovered.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The Paid Acquisition Treadmill: company achieves growth by scaling paid social
spend; organic growth never develops; when iOS privacy changes or CPM inflation
hit, CAC doubles overnight and the growth model collapses.
The Freemium Ceiling: free tier generates large user base but conversion to paid
stalls below 2%; free users create support and infrastructure cost with no
corresponding revenue; the company cannot afford to remove the free tier
because it is the primary acquisition channel.
The Physical DTC Margin Collapse: gross margin before returns looks acceptable;
return rates above category benchmark mean realised gross margin is 15–20 points
lower; contribution margin is negative; the business is subsidising customer trial.

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"What percentage of new users in the last 90 days came from organic channels —
not paid social, not influencer, not paid search?" Weak answer: cannot separate
organic from paid; or organic is below 30% with no plan to improve it.
"What is your D30 retention and how does it compare to your D1 and D7?"
Weak answer: only tracks D1; D30 not measured; or D30 is below category benchmark.
"What is your K-factor — for every user who downloads the app, how many
additional users do they invite or refer who convert?" Weak answer: does not
measure viral coefficient; or states it is positive without a number.
"For physical DTC: what is your return rate by category and your gross margin
after returns and fulfilment?" Weak answer: gross margin stated before returns;
return rate not tracked by SKU.

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
Age Appropriate Design Code (ICO): products likely accessed by under-18s must
complete a DPIA and apply age-appropriate design standards — ICO enforcement
active (TikTok £12.7M fine); non-compliant apps face mandatory redesign or market withdrawal.
Online Safety Act 2023: platforms hosting user-generated content with UK users
have new Ofcom-regulated duties of care — non-compliance fines up to £18M or 10% of global turnover.
DMCCA 2024 (Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act): fake reviews,
drip pricing, and subscription trap practices are now explicitly prohibited
with enhanced Trading Standards enforcement powers.

SECTOR 7 — CLIMATE AND IMPACT
Full library: alex_sector_v7_climate.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Product directly reduces, avoids, removes, or measures greenhouse gas emissions,
or addresses a related environmental or social challenge.
THE MOST IMPORTANT CLIMATE DISTINCTION — TYPE A vs TYPE B:
TYPE A — Technology company in climate: software, data, or platform economics.
Gross margin >60%. Scales without asset deployment. Apply SaaS Library +
this sector library. Examples: carbon accounting SaaS, climate risk data,
grid optimisation software, precision agriculture analytics.
TYPE B — Infrastructure or project company in climate: physical asset deployment.
Gross margin <40%. Revenue scales with physical assets. Long payback periods.
Apply project finance and infrastructure fund analysis + this sector library.
Examples: EV charging networks, solar/wind development, battery storage,
green hydrogen production, EV charging hardware.
Misclassifying a Type B company as a Type A commands a false software multiple.
Sub-categories: Clean Energy Technology; Carbon Markets and Measurement;
Sustainable Food / Agriculture; Circular Economy / Waste; Green Buildings;
Sustainable Mobility; Climate Data / Analytics; Impact Tech.

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
Type A — apply SaaS Library benchmarks with:
CO2 avoided / removed per £1M revenue: STRONG >10,000 tCO2e | CONCERN <1,000 tCO2e
Additionality verified: STRONG third-party verified | FAIL self-reported only

Type B — infrastructure benchmarks:
LCOE (Levelised Cost of Energy): STRONG at or below grid parity in target market |
FAIL >30% above grid parity without a contracted subsidy covering the gap
Installed capacity (MW/MWh): progress vs deployment plan — behind plan by >20%
is a CONCERN; >50% is a FAIL signal on execution capability
Payback period: STRONG <7 years | CONCERN 10–15 years | FAIL >20 years
(must align with investor type — infrastructure fund can accept 15 years;
VC cannot)
Subsidy dependency ratio (revenue from subsidies / total revenue):
STRONG <20% | ACCEPTABLE 20–40% | CONCERN 40–60% | FAIL >60% with no
cliff date visibility

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
CRITICAL — Policy Dependency: business model fails if a specific subsidy, tax
credit, or regulatory mandate is removed — stress-test against a policy reversal
scenario before any investment decision.
CRITICAL — Carbon Credit Quality: voluntary carbon credits without verified
additionality, permanence, and ICVCM Core Carbon Principles compliance are
exposed to the post-2023 quality crisis; credits may be worth zero.
HIGH — Subsidy Cliff Date Visible: subsidy or CfD contract expires within the
investment horizon with no demonstrated path to subsidy independence.
HIGH — Infrastructure Company Mispositioned as Tech: company presents Type B
hardware/infrastructure economics as software (high gross margin claimed without
decomposition); Northvolt (£15B raised, bankruptcy 2024) and Arrival (SPAC at
£10B, near-zero collapse) are the definitive cautionary examples.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The Subsidy Cliff: government policy changes or subsidy contract expires;
company has not reached subsidy independence; revenue drops 40–60% with no
replacement mechanism.
The Carbon Credit Quality Crisis: voluntary carbon portfolio is invalidated
by additionality challenge or post-issuance review; credits cannot be retired
by corporate buyers; revenue collapses.
The Hardware Scale-Up Cost Spiral: hardware unit economics at pilot scale
look viable; production scaling reveals BoM costs do not fall as projected;
gross margin at volume is 20+ points below model.

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"If the [specific subsidy / policy / carbon price] changed by 30%, what happens
to your revenue?" Weak answer: has not modelled this; or assumes the policy
will not change.
"Are your carbon credits verified under ICVCM Core Carbon Principles and do
they meet additionality and permanence standards?" Weak answer: credits are
VCS or Gold Standard registered but additionality has not been independently
assessed post-2023.
"What is your subsidy dependency ratio and when is your earliest subsidy
cliff date?" Weak answer: does not track this metric; or cannot name the cliff date.
"Who verifies your impact metrics and are they independently audited?"
Weak answer: self-reported impact figures; no third-party verification; the
metric definition changes between funding rounds.

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
UK ETS and CBAM: UK Emissions Trading Scheme carbon pricing (£35–45/tonne)
and Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (EU, phased 2026–2034) affect cost
structures for manufacturing and energy-intensive climate companies.
CSRD (EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive): mandatory sustainability
reporting for large EU companies from 2025, mid-cap from 2026 — creates
enterprise buyer demand for climate data and measurement tools; also imposes
supply chain reporting obligations that affect UK climate companies selling into EU.
Biodiversity Net Gain (mandatory England, February 2024): development projects
must deliver 10% biodiversity net gain — creates compliance market for habitat
measurement and BNG unit trading (£20K–£100K per BNG unit).

SECTOR 8 — ENTERPRISE SOFTWARE AND INFRASTRUCTURE
Full library: alex_sector_v8_enterprise.txt
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. CLASSIFICATION SIGNALS
Primary buyer is procurement, IT, legal, or C-suite at a large organisation.
ACV £100K+. Sales cycle 6+ months. Contract includes custom legal terms,
DPA, enterprise SLA, or multi-year commitment. Procurement involves security
review, IT due diligence, legal sign-off, and compliance certification check.
THE MOST IMPORTANT ENTERPRISE QUESTION: Land-and-Expand vs Full Deployment?
Land-and-Expand: initial ACV £50–200K; expansion ACV potential £500K–5M+;
NRR should reach 120–150%+ if the product works.
Full Deployment: initial ACV £500K+; implementation failure is catastrophic
with no beachhead fallback.
Sub-categories: ERP and Core Systems (longest sales cycles, highest switching
cost); Security and Compliance (non-discretionary budget, technical PoC required);
Data and Analytics Infrastructure (usage-based pricing — committed vs consumption
ARR must be separated); Developer Tools / DevOps (PLG-to-enterprise motion);
Communication and Collaboration (per-seat pricing, Microsoft/Google incumbent
risk); Supply Chain and Operations (ROI-led sales, high implementation complexity);
Vertical Enterprise (finite buyer universe — apply Enterprise Only Trap test).

2. CRITICAL METRIC BENCHMARKS
NRR (Series A): STRONG >125% | FAIL <100% (land-and-expand is not working)
Logo churn:     STRONG <3% annual | FAIL >12%
Professional services % of revenue: STRONG <20% | FAIL >45% (services trap risk)
Auto-renewal rate: STRONG >90% | FAIL <75% (product not embedded enough)
Win rate (competitive): STRONG >35% | FAIL <10%
Sales cycle (all sub-categories): FAIL >18 months average (sales process broken)
Software gross margin (decomposed from blended): STRONG >75% (Series A) | FAIL <60%

3. TOP 4 RED FLAGS
HIGH — Sales Cycle Consistently Exceeding 18 Months: average cycle above 18 months
means ARR additions cannot be reliably planned — pipeline dating collapses.
HIGH — No SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 for Product Handling Sensitive Enterprise Data:
binary procurement blocker for Fortune 500, FTSE 100, regulated finance,
NHS, and government — deal stalls at security review indefinitely.
HIGH — G-Cloud Listing Absent for a Public Sector Product: without G-Cloud listing,
UK public sector deals cannot close below £2M in any efficient timeline —
the pipeline claims are not credible.
HIGH — Professional Services Above 40% of Total Revenue: business is partially
a services company; blended gross margin masks collapsed software economics.

4. TOP 3 FAILURE PATTERNS
The Implementation Crisis: product works but implementation takes 3x the
contracted timeline; buying champion is replaced mid-implementation; new
executive does not renew — one failed implementation consumes 20–30% of
operating capacity and often destroys the customer relationship.
The Services Trap: each enterprise customer requires bespoke implementation;
professional services headcount grows faster than engineering; services
revenue crosses 40% of total; the company cannot raise at a software multiple.
The Land Without Expand: initial deals close; the product delivers value
in the beachhead; NRR stays at 97–103% for two years; the land-and-expand
model that justified the investment thesis never activates; all growth
requires new logo acquisition at full enterprise sales cost.

5. TOP 4 FOUNDER QUESTIONS
"What is your average sales cycle from first meeting to signed contract,
and has it changed in the last 12 months?" Weak answer: cannot state a
number; or sales cycle is lengthening without explanation.
"For your last 5 customers who went live — contracted implementation timeline
vs actual time to go live?" Weak answer: cannot name go-live dates;
one or more customers still in implementation after 12+ months.
"What percentage of your renewals auto-renew vs require active re-sell?"
Weak answer: does not track auto-renewal separately from NRR.
"Are you listed on G-Cloud and do you have SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001?"
Weak answer: neither in place; both "in progress" without a certification date.

6. KEY REGULATORY BLOCKERS
G-Cloud (Crown Commercial Service): mandatory procurement route for UK public
sector cloud software — without listing, government and NHS deals cannot
close efficiently; applications open twice per year.
DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act, EU, January 2025): enterprise software
sold to EU financial institutions must include DORA-compliant contract terms
(uptime SLA, 4-hour incident notification, audit rights, sub-contractor disclosure)
— non-compliant terms block finserv procurement across the EU.
Cyber Essentials Plus: required for UK government contracts handling sensitive
data, MOD supply chain, and many NHS trust vendor approvals — binary
procurement requirement, not optional.
