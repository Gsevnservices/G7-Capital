CATEGORY 1: FINANCIAL RED FLAGS
Version 1.0. Built: April 2026.
15 flags. Apply mechanically to every deal submission.
These flags are observable from a submission without a meeting.
Two analysts applying these rules reach the same conclusion.

APPLICATION RULE:
For every deal submission, Alex checks all 15 flags before completing
the screening note. Flags are not discretionary — if the signal is
present, the flag is raised. Flags are then weighed in aggregate.
One CRITICAL flag = automatic deep investigation required.
Two or more HIGH flags = note this in the Risk Assessment section.
CRITICAL flag + confirmed by verification = strong PASS presumption.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F01: REVENUE CONCENTRATION — TOP CUSTOMER ABOVE THRESHOLD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH (single customer 30–50% of ARR) | CRITICAL (single customer above 50% of ARR)

SIGNAL:
Submission states, implies, or allows calculation that one named or unnamed customer
represents 30% or more of current ARR — either through explicit disclosure, named
case study revenue figures, or customer count vs ARR that implies concentration.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A single customer above 30% of ARR creates a business with two distinct failure modes
running simultaneously: (1) the customer can extract price concessions at renewal because
the vendor cannot afford to lose them — NRR figures from this account are not negotiated
at arm's length; (2) if the customer churns, misses an expansion, or is acquired and
consolidates vendors, the company misses its ARR plan by 30%+ in a single event.
Above 50%, the company is operationally a single-customer relationship with a startup
attached. All prior growth metrics, NRR, and expansion data are dominated by one
relationship and tell Alex almost nothing about the underlying product-market fit.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The single customer represents more than 50% of ARR AND the company is pre-Series A
AND the customer has not yet committed to a multi-year renewal AND there are no other
customers with ACV above £20K. All four conditions together = PASS on concentration grounds.
At Series A and beyond: single customer above 40% of ARR is disqualifying unless a
signed multi-year contract is in place locking in at least 24 months of revenue at
the current or higher ACV.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The concentrated customer has a signed multi-year contract (24+ months) with a
stated minimum ACV, eliminating single-event churn risk for the investment horizon.
(b) The customer is a household-name enterprise account and the relationship is actively
expanding — concentration is a consequence of product success, not customer scarcity.
(c) The company has 3+ other customers at ACV above £50K and the concentration customer
was the first — concentration is a timing artefact of early sales motion, not structural.
(d) The company is deliberately a single-enterprise deployment product (e.g. bespoke
infrastructure for one regulated institution) — in which case it is not a SaaS business
and should be assessed on different terms.

VERIFICATION:
Request ARR waterfall by customer for the last 12 months: customer name (or anonymised
reference), ACV at start of period, expansions, contractions, end ACV. Calculate top
customer as percentage of total ending ARR. Ask: "If your largest customer did not renew,
what would your NRR be?" A company that cannot answer this has not modelled its own
concentration risk.

PATTERN LESSON:
When a single customer represents more than 30% of ARR, calculate what the company's
growth rate and NRR look like excluding that customer before accepting any headline metric.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F02: ADJUSTED METRICS WITHOUT DISCLOSURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
Submission presents "adjusted EBITDA," "adjusted gross margin," "adjusted burn,"
"contribution margin," or any profitability metric prefixed with "adjusted,"
"normalised," "pro forma," or "excluding" — without explicitly disclosing every
line item being excluded and the total value of exclusions.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Adjusted metrics are not inherently fraudulent, but they are the most common vector
for early-stage financial obfuscation. The most frequent exclusions: stock-based
compensation (real economic cost to existing shareholders), founder salaries below
market rate (cost structure is understated relative to a scaled business),
one-time costs described as exceptional but recurring annually, and customer
acquisition costs allocated to a separate marketing budget line that is excluded
from the reported CAC. When exclusions are undisclosed, Alex cannot verify whether
the adjustment is legitimate or whether the unadjusted metric is worse by a small
amount or by a factor of 3.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The submission presents only adjusted metrics with no GAAP or unadjusted equivalent
disclosed, AND the adjustments are not individually listed with values, AND the company
is at Series A or beyond (where financial reporting standards should be established).
At pre-seed and seed stage, partial financial reporting is common — apply lower
severity (MEDIUM) and request the underlying figures.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) Every exclusion is listed individually with the value: "Adjusted EBITDA excludes
£1.2M SBC, £0.4M one-time legal settlement, and £0.3M office relocation costs —
unadjusted EBITDA is -£2.1M."
(b) The adjustment is standard for the business model (e.g. gross merchandise value
vs net revenue in a marketplace — a disclosed adjustment, not a hidden one).
(c) The submission provides both adjusted and unadjusted figures and explains the
business rationale for the adjustment in the context of investor decision-making.

VERIFICATION:
Request the income statement or management accounts in unadjusted form for the last
4 quarters. List each item excluded from the adjusted figure and confirm its value.
Calculate the unadjusted equivalent of every adjusted metric presented. If the company
cannot produce an unadjusted figure, the adjusted metric is unverifiable and should
not be accepted as evidence of financial performance.

PATTERN LESSON:
When a submission uses any adjusted financial metric, request the unadjusted equivalent
and the complete list of exclusions before using the metric in the screening analysis.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F03: ARR INFLATION — PILOTS AND POCs INCLUDED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
Submission states an ARR figure that, when cross-referenced with disclosed customer
count, named customer logos, pipeline description, or stage of commercialisation,
implies inclusion of pilots, POCs, LOIs, verbal commitments, or free trials — i.e.
revenue that is not yet contracted, signed, and invoiced at a stated annual rate.

SIGNALS THAT INDICATE INFLATION:
— "ARR includes our pilot with [enterprise name] which converts in Q3"
— ARR per customer implied by ARR ÷ customer count is above stated ACV
— Customer count described as "paying and evaluating" without separation
— Pipeline conversion event described as "when X signs, ARR becomes Y"
— ARR growth between the last two disclosure dates outpaces the number of
  new contracts described in the same period

WHAT IT MEANS:
ARR means Annual Recurring Revenue: contracted, signed, invoiced revenue that will
recur for at least 12 months unless the customer actively cancels. Pilots, POCs,
and LOIs are pipeline, not ARR. A company that inflates ARR by 20–40% through
inclusion of non-contracted relationships will report false growth rates, false
NRR figures (if expansions of pilots are counted), and false CAC payback periods.
All downstream metrics built on the inflated ARR number are unreliable.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The ARR figure includes any revenue from a customer that has not signed a commercial
agreement with a stated minimum commitment and invoice schedule AND the inflation
represents more than 15% of total stated ARR AND the company is at Series A or beyond.
At seed stage with ARR below £500K: any pilot inclusion that represents more than
25% of the stated figure is disqualifying.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The submission clearly separates contracted ARR from pipeline ARR and the flag
applies only to the pipeline figure, which is labelled as such.
(b) A pilot is structured as a paid pilot with a contractual conversion obligation
at a stated rate — this is contracted revenue, not a pilot in the usual sense.
(c) The inflation is small (below 10% of total ARR) and is explicitly disclosed.

VERIFICATION:
Request a signed contract or purchase order for each customer included in the ARR
figure. Ask: "Can you send me the signed agreement and first invoice for each of
your top 5 ARR contributors?" Any customer without a signed agreement is removed
from ARR for the purposes of the screening analysis, and the corrected ARR is
used in all subsequent metric calculations.

PATTERN LESSON:
When ARR per customer implied by the submission materially exceeds the stated ACV
range, or when pipeline events are described as affecting ARR, assume ARR is inflated
and request signed contracts for the top 5 revenue contributors before proceeding.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F04: DECLINING GROSS MARGIN TREND
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
Submission discloses gross margin figures across multiple periods — quarterly or
annually — showing a decline of 3 or more percentage points over 3 or more consecutive
quarters; or a current gross margin is materially below the sector benchmark with
no explanation offered.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Gross margin is the foundation of every other profitability metric. A declining gross
margin trend in a software business predicts one or more of four structural problems:
(1) AI inference costs rising faster than revenue as usage scales; (2) professional
services headcount growing to support customer implementations — services are being
added to retain customers who would otherwise churn; (3) pricing is being discounted
at renewal to retain at-risk customers — effective ACV is falling while listed ACV
is unchanged; (4) COGS line items (cloud hosting, data costs, support) are scaling
faster than revenue, indicating a business model without operating leverage.
A SaaS business should have expanding gross margins as it scales. Declining margins
contradict the fundamental economics of the business model.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Gross margin has declined more than 8 percentage points over 4 consecutive quarters
AND the company cannot identify the specific COGS driver causing the decline AND is
claiming a valuation multiple consistent with a high-margin software business.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The decline is explicitly attributed to a planned and temporary increase in
implementation costs for a cohort of new enterprise customers — with a stated date
by which margins will recover as those customers go live.
(b) The decline reflects a deliberate pricing strategy to win market share in a
competitive window, with a stated plan to return to target margins within 12 months.
(c) The business is early-stage and margin is volatile due to small absolute revenue —
a move from 71% to 68% across three quarters in a £200K ARR business is noise,
not a structural signal.

VERIFICATION:
Request quarterly P&L for the last 6 quarters with COGS decomposed into specific
line items: hosting/infrastructure, third-party APIs, customer support, implementation
and professional services, and other. Identify which line item is growing as a
percentage of revenue. The driver of the margin decline determines whether it is
structural or temporary.

PATTERN LESSON:
When gross margin has declined for 3 or more consecutive quarters, request the
COGS breakdown and identify the specific line item causing the compression before
accepting the company's explanation of the trend.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F05: BURN MULTIPLE ABOVE 2x AT SERIES A AND BEYOND
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH (2–3x) | CRITICAL (above 4x with declining growth)

SIGNAL:
Net burn divided by net new ARR for the trailing 12 months exceeds 2.0x —
i.e. the company is spending more than £2 to generate each £1 of net new ARR.
Observable from submission when both burn rate and ARR growth are disclosed,
or calculable from headcount × average fully-loaded cost vs net ARR adds.

BURN MULTIPLE CALCULATION:
Net burn (12 months) ÷ Net new ARR (12 months) = Burn Multiple
Net new ARR = ending ARR − beginning ARR + churned ARR
(This is the correct denominator — it includes expansion and accounts for churn)

WHAT IT MEANS:
Burn multiple measures the capital efficiency of growth. A burn multiple above 2x
means the company is in an unsustainable growth mode: it is buying revenue rather
than earning it. Companies with burn multiples above 3x at Series A almost never
improve to below 1.5x without a painful cost restructuring. The pattern: high burn
multiple is caused by over-hiring ahead of revenue (sales team, CS team, engineering)
on the assumption that ARR will follow — when ARR growth disappoints, the headcount
is fixed and the burn multiple rises further. The correction requires layoffs,
which damage morale and slow the very growth the company needs.
Above 4x with declining growth: the company is in a death spiral where each quarter
of declining growth makes the burn multiple worse, which requires more capital,
which runs out before growth recovers.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Burn multiple above 4x for two consecutive quarters AND YoY ARR growth is declining
AND the company is raising its next round at a higher valuation than the previous —
the growth story is not supported by the capital efficiency, and the valuation is
not sustainable on the current trajectory.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) Burn multiple above 2x at pre-seed or seed stage where absolute burn is small
(below £150K/month) and the spending is on founding team and product development
ahead of first revenue — this is expected, not alarming.
(b) Burn multiple temporarily elevated due to a deliberate market-entry investment
(e.g. hiring a 10-person enterprise sales team ahead of a pipeline that is 60 days
from closing) — must be temporary with a stated date by which multiple normalises.
(c) Industry-specific capital intensity: deep tech, biotech, or hardware companies
where pre-revenue capital deployment is structural — apply the Deep Tech framework
instead and use capital-to-milestone ratio as the primary metric.

VERIFICATION:
Calculate burn multiple from submission data. If not directly calculable, estimate:
headcount × £8K average fully-loaded monthly cost = monthly burn estimate.
Net new ARR = current ARR − ARR 12 months ago. If the company provides a different
burn figure, request the monthly bank statement cash balance for the last 12 months
to verify net cash consumed.

PATTERN LESSON:
When burn multiple exceeds 2x, verify whether growth rate is accelerating or
decelerating — accelerating growth at 2x burn is a growth investment; decelerating
growth at 2x burn is a structural efficiency problem that compounds.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F06: RUNWAY BELOW 12 MONTHS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: CRITICAL (below 6 months) | HIGH (6–12 months)

SIGNAL:
Cash on hand divided by monthly net burn produces a runway figure below 12 months —
observable when both cash balance and burn rate are stated, or when the last funding
date, amount raised, and monthly burn allow estimation of current cash position.

RUNWAY ESTIMATION FROM SUBMISSION:
If cash balance not stated: (Last raise amount) − (months since raise × monthly burn)
= estimated current cash. If burn rate not stated: headcount × £8K/month estimate.
Flag this estimation in the note and request confirmation.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A company with less than 12 months of runway is in active fundraising mode whether
or not it acknowledges this. The dynamics of a sub-12-month runway deal:
(1) The founder's primary job is fundraising, not building — execution slows.
(2) Customers sense urgency and defer decisions — "let's see if they survive" is an
unspoken procurement response to a startup clearly under financial pressure.
(3) The company may accept any terms from an investor because the alternative is zero.
(4) Key hires reject offers because runway uncertainty makes offers unattractive.
Below 6 months: the company cannot complete a standard venture fundraise (diligence +
term sheet + close typically takes 3–6 months). A bridge or emergency capital event
is the only realistic option — which almost always happens at a punitive valuation.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Runway is below 6 months AND the company is not yet in active fundraising AND has
no signed term sheets AND has no existing investor committed to a bridge. This is a
situation where any investment from a new investor would likely be diluted by a
bridge from an existing investor who has priority — the deal terms are not investable
without full cap table transparency.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The company is already in a signed term sheet or final close process with a
lead investor — the runway is short because the round is imminent, not because
the company is in distress.
(b) The company has reached profitability or cash flow breakeven at current burn —
the 12-month runway calculation is based on historical burn that is no longer the
operating rate because the business is now self-sustaining.
(c) The company has a signed revenue contract or grant that will extend runway by
6+ months within 30 days — the timing of the cash event is certain.

VERIFICATION:
Request the most recent bank statement showing cash balance and the most recent
monthly management accounts showing net cash consumed. Ask: "At your current burn
rate, when does the company run out of cash if this round does not close?" A founder
who cannot answer this question precisely is not managing their financial position.

PATTERN LESSON:
When runway is calculable at below 12 months, confirm whether the company is already
in a closing process before assessing the submission — a company that is 60 days from
close with a strong lead is a different risk profile from a company with no active process.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F07: REVENUE RECOGNITION MISMATCH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
Submission states or implies that revenue from multi-year contracts is recognised at
signing (upfront) rather than ratably over the contract period — detectable when:
(a) Total Contract Value (TCV) and Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) are presented as
equivalent or ARR is described as "booked"; (b) revenue in a single quarter
materially exceeds the run-rate implied by the stated ARR; (c) the company
describes "recognising" a multi-year deal at signing as a revenue event.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Subscription and SaaS revenue should be recognised ratably — one-twelfth per month
of the annual contract value, over the contract period. A company that recognises a
3-year £300K contract as £300K revenue at signing is reporting £300K revenue where
the correct IFRS 15 / UK GAAP figure is £8.3K per month. This creates three problems:
(1) The reported revenue in the signing quarter is artificially high and the following
quarters will show a "decline" in revenue that is purely an accounting artefact.
(2) The ARR metric (which should reflect annualised recurring revenue) is conflated
with TCV — a fundamentally different measure.
(3) At acquisition or IPO, auditors will require revenue restatement — a company
built on upfront recognition may have to restate 2–3 years of revenue, destroying
the growth story that justified the valuation.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The company is at Series A or beyond AND has not engaged an auditor or accountant
to establish a compliant revenue recognition policy AND cannot produce an ARR schedule
distinct from a TCV schedule. At this stage, the financial infrastructure required
to raise institutional capital does not exist.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The company explicitly separates TCV and ARR in the submission and correctly
defines ARR as the annualised value of current-period contracted recurring revenue.
(b) The business model legitimately recognises revenue upfront because the service
is fully delivered at a point in time (e.g. a one-time implementation with no
recurring component) — this is not a mismatch, it is a different business model.
(c) The company has a Big 4 or regional accountancy firm reviewing its revenue
recognition and can confirm IFRS 15 compliance on request.

VERIFICATION:
Request an ARR schedule showing: (a) customer name, (b) contract start date,
(c) contract end date, (d) total contract value, (e) ARR (annualised value).
If TCV and ARR are different figures for multi-year contracts, revenue recognition
is correct. If TCV and ARR are the same figure for a 3-year contract, recognition
is wrong and all revenue figures in the submission must be recalculated.

PATTERN LESSON:
When a submission uses the words "booked," "recognised," or "signed" to describe
ARR additions, verify that the ARR figure reflects annualised contract value and not
total contract value before using any revenue metric.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F08: DEFERRED REVENUE DECLINING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: MEDIUM

SIGNAL:
Deferred revenue balance on the balance sheet has declined quarter-on-quarter for
2 or more consecutive quarters — observable when balance sheet data is included in
the submission or management accounts, or when a company describes a shift from
multi-year to monthly/annual contracts in the same period.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Deferred revenue is revenue collected from customers but not yet recognised —
it represents the future revenue the company has already been paid for. A growing
deferred revenue balance is a strong indicator of business health: it means the
company is collecting cash in advance, winning multi-year contracts, and locking in
future revenue. A declining deferred revenue balance means one or more of: fewer
multi-year contracts being signed (customers unwilling to commit longer term);
existing multi-year contracts reaching their end without renewal as multi-year;
or a deliberate shift to monthly billing (which increases churn risk and reduces
future revenue visibility). It is a leading indicator — the revenue decline it
predicts typically shows up 2–4 quarters later.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Deferred revenue has declined for 3 or more consecutive quarters AND the company
has simultaneously shifted from annual prepay to monthly billing AND logo churn is
above 10% annually — all three together indicate customers are no longer willing
to commit to the product at annual terms, which is a retention crisis in early form.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The decline is explained by a single large multi-year contract that ended in the
period — one contract expiry distorts a small deferred revenue base significantly.
(b) The company deliberately shifted to monthly billing for a strategic reason (e.g.
removing the prepayment barrier to accelerate new customer acquisition) and ARR
growth has accelerated as a result.
(c) The deferred revenue balance is very small relative to total ARR (below 5%)
and is therefore not a material forward indicator.

VERIFICATION:
Request the balance sheet for the last 6 quarters and extract the deferred revenue
line. Plot the trend. Ask: "Has your mix of annual vs multi-year contracts changed
over the last 12 months? And what percentage of your ARR is prepaid vs invoiced
monthly?" The answers will confirm whether the trend is structural or situational.

PATTERN LESSON:
When deferred revenue is declining and ARR is growing, request the contract length
mix to determine whether customers are becoming less willing to commit — deferred
revenue is a leading indicator that arrives 2–4 quarters before the ARR impact.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F09: GROSS MARGIN BELOW SECTOR THRESHOLD WITH INCONSISTENT VALUATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
Stated gross margin is materially below the sector benchmark for the claimed
business model — AND the company is seeking or has accepted a valuation multiple
consistent with a high-margin business of its type. The valuation multiple and the
gross margin are structurally inconsistent.

SECTOR GROSS MARGIN THRESHOLDS (MINIMUM FOR SOFTWARE-GRADE MULTIPLE):
Pure SaaS / subscription software: 70% minimum
Marketplace (net revenue):          50% minimum
Fintech (payments, lending blended): 40% minimum
Enterprise software (blended):       60% minimum (software-only: 70%+)
Consumer subscription:               60% minimum
AI-native SaaS (after inference costs): 60% minimum

WHAT IT MEANS:
Valuation multiples for software companies are justified by the expectation that
gross margins will expand toward 80%+ as the business scales. A company with 45%
gross margin claiming a 20x ARR multiple is asking an investor to pay a software
price for a services or hardware economics business. The implied future gross margin
that would need to materialise to justify the multiple may be structurally impossible
given the COGS drivers (inference costs, services headcount, hardware).
This is not a moral failure — it is a modelling error on the part of the founder
or, more concerning, a deliberate framing choice that will correct painfully at
the next funding round when a more rigorous investor analyses the unit economics.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Gross margin is below 50% AND the company is seeking a valuation above 15x ARR
AND cannot identify a specific COGS reduction driver that will increase gross margin
to above 65% within 18 months AND the business model is described as SaaS or software.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The company explicitly acknowledges that its current gross margin reflects its
early stage (implementation-heavy, high support cost) and presents a credible model
showing margin expansion to >70% within 3–4 years as the implementation burden
reduces and the customer base scales.
(b) The company is not a SaaS business — it is a marketplace, fintech, or hardware
business — and the valuation multiple reflects the correct comparable set for
that business model type.
(c) The low gross margin is entirely attributable to a transitional professional
services burden that will be eliminated as a partner implementation ecosystem develops.

VERIFICATION:
Decompose gross margin into its COGS components. Identify which specific line items
are causing the margin to fall below the sector threshold. Model each line item as
a percentage of revenue at 3x the current ARR. If the structural margin at scale
is still below 65% for a claimed SaaS business, the valuation multiple is not
justified by the gross margin trajectory.

PATTERN LESSON:
When gross margin is below the sector threshold, calculate what the gross margin
will be at 3x current ARR under each COGS assumption before assessing whether the
requested valuation multiple is supportable.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F10: CUSTOMER ACQUISITION COST RISING WHILE LTV IS FLAT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
Submission discloses or allows calculation of CAC across multiple periods — showing
an increase of 20% or more over 2 or more consecutive quarters — while LTV (or its
proxy: ACV × gross margin ÷ logo churn rate) is flat or declining over the same period.

CAC ESTIMATION WHEN NOT DIRECTLY DISCLOSED:
(Total sales and marketing spend in period) ÷ (New customers acquired in period) = CAC
If sales and marketing spend is not separately disclosed: headcount in sales + marketing
roles × £8K fully-loaded monthly cost = estimated sales and marketing spend.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A rising CAC with flat LTV means the LTV/CAC ratio is contracting — the fundamental
unit economics of customer acquisition are deteriorating. The most common causes:
(1) Early channel saturation — the cheapest, highest-intent customers have been
acquired and the company is now reaching lower-quality audiences at higher cost.
(2) Competitive pressure — new entrants or incumbent responses have raised the cost
of paid acquisition or lengthened sales cycles (time-based CAC increase).
(3) ICP drift — the company has moved upmarket or changed its target customer without
updating its acquisition model, and the new customer type costs more to acquire.
(4) Declining referral and organic contribution — early customers were acquired through
founder network and word-of-mouth; this channel is exhausting and is being replaced by
more expensive paid channels without a corresponding LTV increase. This is the early
stage of the CAC Spiral failure pattern.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
CAC has increased more than 40% over 4 consecutive quarters AND LTV/CAC ratio is
below 2x AND the company cannot identify the specific channel or driver causing the
increase AND is projecting LTV/CAC improvement without a structural channel change planned.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The CAC increase is attributable to a deliberate move upmarket — ACV has increased
proportionally with CAC, maintaining the LTV/CAC ratio.
(b) The CAC increase is a temporary effect of hiring a new sales team (fixed cost
increase in sales salaries without a corresponding immediate ARR increase) — and the
pipeline build from the new team is visible and quantifiable.
(c) The increase is within the natural statistical noise for a company with fewer
than 5 new customers per quarter — small denominators create volatile CAC measurements.

VERIFICATION:
Request a channel-by-channel CAC breakdown for the last 4 quarters: paid search, paid
social, events/outbound, inbound/organic, partner/referral, founder network. Identify
which channels have increased in cost and which have increased in volume. A CAC increase
in paid channels accompanied by growth in organic channels is less alarming than a
CAC increase with organic channel decline.

PATTERN LESSON:
When CAC is rising and the company attributes it to a planned investment in growth,
verify that LTV/CAC ratio is maintained — if LTV has not increased with CAC, the
investment in growth is destroying unit economics, not funding them.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F11: NET REVENUE RETENTION DECLINING TREND
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
NRR has declined more than 5 percentage points over 2 or more consecutive quarters —
even if the current NRR remains above 100%. Detectable when the submission discloses
NRR across multiple periods, or when the ratio of net new ARR from expansion vs new
logos shifts materially quarter-on-quarter.

WHAT IT MEANS:
A declining NRR trend is more alarming than a low NRR in isolation, because it
indicates the trajectory is moving in the wrong direction. A company at 115% NRR
declining to 107% over two quarters will likely be at 99% within two more —
at which point the business requires all new logo ARR just to maintain flat total ARR.
The causes of NRR decline typically precede visible churn by 2–4 quarters:
(1) Expansion ARR from existing customers is slowing — the product has delivered
its initial value and customers are not finding incremental use cases to pay for.
(2) A cohort of early-adopter customers is reaching its first renewal — the initial
cohort, acquired under conditions more favourable than today's market, is churning
at a higher rate than the reported average.
(3) A competitive product is being evaluated at renewal — customers are not churning
immediately but are reducing their contract scope while they evaluate alternatives.
(4) Pricing concessions are being granted at renewal — the ACV of existing customers
is declining even when the logo count is not.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
NRR has declined more than 10 percentage points over 3 consecutive quarters AND the
decline is driven by both contraction and churn (not just slower expansion) AND the
company does not have a documented customer success programme with QBR cadences
that would catch retention risk before renewal.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The NRR decline is explained by the graduation of one or two large early customers
from an introductory pricing tier to a standard tier — a one-time compression event.
(b) The business deliberately reduced its enterprise ACV threshold to expand the
addressable customer base — lower individual ACVs temporarily suppress NRR while
the new ICP cohort builds.
(c) The NRR decline is within 3 percentage points over 2 quarters in a business with
fewer than 20 customers — small cohort statistical noise.

VERIFICATION:
Request NRR broken down into its components for the last 4 quarters: (a) gross renewal
rate (% of expiring ACV that renews at the same or higher value), (b) expansion ARR
from existing customers, (c) contraction ARR from existing customers. Identify which
component is driving the decline — gross renewal deterioration is more alarming than
slower expansion.

PATTERN LESSON:
When NRR is declining across consecutive periods, decompose into gross renewal rate
and expansion rate separately — gross renewal rate decline indicates a retention crisis;
expansion rate decline indicates a product depth problem — each requires a different response.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F12: PAYABLES CONCENTRATION — SINGLE SUPPLIER ABOVE 40% OF COGS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: MEDIUM

SIGNAL:
A single vendor, cloud provider, API supplier, or infrastructure partner is responsible
for more than 40% of cost of goods sold — detectable when the submission mentions
a specific infrastructure dependency (e.g. "our product runs on AWS," "all AI inference
is via OpenAI API," "our banking infrastructure is provided by [BaaS partner]") where
the cost of that dependency is implied to be the dominant COGS line item.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Supplier concentration creates a mirror-image risk to customer concentration.
Three specific failure modes:
(1) The supplier raises prices — the vendor has no negotiating power and cannot switch
without a major engineering effort. Gross margin compresses immediately.
(2) The supplier changes its terms, deprecates an API, or exits the market — the vendor
must rebuild a critical dependency in an emergency, consuming engineering capacity
at the worst possible time and creating customer-facing service disruption.
(3) The supplier acquires a competitor or enters the vendor's market directly —
the vendor is simultaneously dependent on and in competition with the same company.
The most common instance in 2025: AI-native SaaS products where OpenAI inference
represents 40–60% of COGS — OpenAI's pricing decisions are a direct determinant of
the vendor's gross margin.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
A single supplier represents more than 60% of COGS AND the supplier has raised prices
in the last 12 months AND the company has no contractual protection against further
price increases AND there is no viable alternative supplier that could be integrated
within 6 months.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The dependency is on a commodity infrastructure provider (AWS, GCP, Azure) where
pricing is stable, alternatives exist, and the switching cost is high but achievable.
(b) The company has a negotiated enterprise agreement with the supplier that locks in
pricing for 24+ months, removing the near-term price risk.
(c) The company is actively building a multi-model or multi-supplier architecture that
will reduce concentration below 40% within 12 months — and engineering time is already
allocated to this work.

VERIFICATION:
Request the COGS breakdown and identify the top 3 suppliers by cost. Ask: "If [named
supplier] doubled their prices tomorrow, what would your gross margin be? And how long
would it take you to migrate to an alternative?" The answer reveals both the financial
exposure and the operational dependency level.

PATTERN LESSON:
When a product's COGS is dominated by a single API provider or infrastructure partner,
model the gross margin impact of a 50% price increase from that supplier before accepting
the stated margin as the sustainable operating figure.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F13: RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS — UNDISCLOSED OR UNDERPLAYED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: HIGH

SIGNAL:
A significant portion of revenue — more than 10% — comes from customers with a
direct relationship to the founding team: founders' previous employers, founders'
family businesses, co-founders' other ventures, investors' own portfolio companies,
or any entity where the purchasing decision was influenced by a personal or financial
relationship rather than arm's-length commercial evaluation. Detectable from: named
customer logos that overlap with founder biography; customer descriptions that use
the word "relationship" rather than "sales process"; or ARR growth that is
disproportionate to the sales infrastructure that exists to generate it.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Related party revenue is not inherently fraudulent — a founder's previous employer
becoming a customer is a legitimate first reference. But it is not evidence of
product-market fit. A buyer who purchases because of a personal relationship rather
than a commercial evaluation is: (a) more likely to accept below-market pricing;
(b) less likely to be a replicable acquisition — the channel cannot be scaled;
(c) unlikely to be an objective reference — they will not give a balanced view
of the product to prospective customers. If related party revenue represents more
than 20% of ARR, the commercial traction is materially overstated.
More seriously: undisclosed related party transactions suggest a founder who is
not transparent about their business — which is the most important character signal
Alex can observe from a submission.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Related party transactions are identified and were not disclosed in the submission
AND represent more than 20% of ARR AND the affected customers are being used as
reference accounts or evidence of commercial traction in the submission materials.
The lack of disclosure is disqualifying independent of the amount.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The related party transaction is explicitly disclosed, the ACV is at or above
market rate (confirmed by the pricing of independent customers), and the related
party customer is not being cited as evidence of arm's-length commercial traction.
(b) The revenue is a small portion (below 5% of ARR) and is explicitly described
as a founder-facilitated early sale — a starting point, not a proof point.

VERIFICATION:
Cross-reference named customer logos with founder LinkedIn profiles, prior employment,
and known investor portfolio. Search company names in the submission against the
founders' professional history. If a match is identified, ask directly: "Is [customer]
a company where you or a co-founder has a personal or professional relationship?"
An honest answer resolves the flag. Evasion does not.

PATTERN LESSON:
When named customers cannot be verified as arm's-length through public information,
ask directly whether any revenue comes from relationships with the founding team
before using that revenue as evidence of commercial traction.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F14: CAPITALISED R&D MASKING BURN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: MEDIUM

SIGNAL:
Submission discloses a capitalised R&D or software development cost line item on
the balance sheet — OR the company reports an EBITDA or operating loss that is
materially lower than cash burn, in a company without significant fixed asset
purchases — suggesting that development costs are being capitalised rather than
expensed in the period incurred.

DETECTION METHOD:
Cash burn − Reported operating loss > £200K per quarter in a company without
significant CapEx: the gap is likely capitalised R&D. If the company mentions
"capitalised development costs" or "intangible assets — software" on the balance
sheet, this confirms the practice.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Under IFRS and UK GAAP, software development costs that meet specific criteria
(technical feasibility established, intention to complete, ability to use or sell)
can be capitalised as an intangible asset and amortised. This is a legitimate
accounting treatment. However, early-stage companies that capitalise R&D reduce
their reported operating expenses — making the business appear closer to profitability
than it actually is on a cash basis. The effect: reported EBITDA may show -£500K
while actual cash consumed is -£1.2M. An investor using the reported loss to
estimate burn multiple or runway will underestimate both. At exit or acquisition,
the capitalised R&D will be scrutinised — if it does not meet the strict criteria
for capitalisation, it will be written off, reducing net assets and potentially
triggering a goodwill impairment on prior-period accounts.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
Capitalised R&D represents more than 25% of total reported operating expenses AND
the company is presenting EBITDA-based burn metrics to investors without disclosing
that cash burn is materially higher AND the company is at Series A or beyond where
institutional accounting standards should be applied.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The capitalisation is explicitly disclosed and the submission provides both
the reported (GAAP) operating loss and the cash burn figure separately.
(b) The capitalised amounts relate to a clearly identifiable product development
project that is commercially deployed and generating revenue — the accounting
treatment is defensible.
(c) The amounts are small relative to total expenses and the effect on burn
multiple calculation is below one-quarter turn.

VERIFICATION:
Request the full P&L and balance sheet for the last 4 quarters. Identify the
capitalised R&D line on the balance sheet. Calculate actual cash burn: operating
loss + amortisation of capitalised development + any other non-cash charges.
Use cash burn, not operating loss, for all efficiency metric calculations.

PATTERN LESSON:
When reported operating loss is materially lower than disclosed monthly cash burn,
request the balance sheet to identify capitalised R&D and recalculate all efficiency
metrics using the cash-basis burn figure.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
FLAG F15: CASH FLOW POSITIVE BUT ACCRUAL NEGATIVE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
CATEGORY: Financial
SEVERITY: MEDIUM

SIGNAL:
Submission describes the company as "cash flow positive" or "cash generative"
while simultaneously disclosing a net income or operating loss that is materially
negative — a gap that is unexplained by non-cash items (depreciation, amortisation,
or stock-based compensation). OR the company explicitly states that cash flow
turned positive in a specific quarter while ARR growth did not accelerate.

WHAT IT MEANS:
Positive cash flow while accrual-basis profitability is negative occurs in two
legitimate scenarios and two misleading ones.

LEGITIMATE — Deferred revenue drawdown: a subscription business collects cash
annually in advance and recognises it monthly. The business is cash positive because
customers pay upfront but accrual-negative because revenue is recognised ratably.
This is healthy and expected in prepaid SaaS.

LEGITIMATE — Accounts payable extension: a business temporarily delays paying
its suppliers, generating cash inflow. This is a one-time cash management tactic,
not recurring cash generation.

MISLEADING — Deferred revenue drawdown masking decline: the company is cash positive
because it is drawing down a stock of previously collected deferred revenue — but new
deferred revenue additions are below the drawdown rate. The cash positive position
will reverse when the deferred revenue pool is exhausted.

MISLEADING — Delayed payables signalling supplier stress: the company is not paying
suppliers on time because it cannot — the apparent cash positivity is a precursor to
a payables crisis.

DISQUALIFYING WHEN:
The company describes itself as "cash flow positive" as evidence of financial health
in a fundraising context AND deferred revenue has been declining for 2+ quarters
(indicating the cash positive position is temporary drawdown) AND accrual-basis
losses are more than 2x operating cash flow positive — the accounting basis of
the "positive" claim is misleading to investors.

MANAGEABLE WHEN:
(a) The cash flow positive position is correctly attributed to prepaid annual
contracts in the submission — the company explicitly acknowledges it is cash
positive due to advance collections, not accrual profitability.
(b) The cash flow positive position is accompanied by growing deferred revenue
— meaning new prepayments are exceeding existing drawdowns, which is a genuine
positive signal.

VERIFICATION:
Request the cash flow statement for the last 4 quarters broken into: (a) operating
cash flow, (b) change in deferred revenue, (c) change in accounts receivable,
(d) change in accounts payable. If operating cash flow is positive but deferred
revenue is declining, the business is drawing down pre-collected cash, not generating
genuine operating cash flow. Ask: "Will you be cash flow positive next quarter if
no new annual contracts are signed?"

PATTERN LESSON:
When a company claims to be cash flow positive, verify whether the positive cash
position is driven by growing deferred revenue (healthy) or declining deferred
revenue drawdown (temporary and declining) before using cash flow positivity as
evidence of financial health.
